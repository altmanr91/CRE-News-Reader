CMBS Conduit Capital Focuses Heavily on Multifamily, Office Sectors

Commercial Observer — Mon, 21 Sep 2026 13:02:13 +0000

SUMMARY

Market Research / CMBS Data

The latest conduit CMBS data from CRED iQ indicates a lending market that has become more selective rather than more cautious, with multifamily and office sectors emerging as exceptions to the trend of increased equity requirements for borrowers. Multifamily loans saw an increase in loan-to-value (LTV) to 62% and a significant drop in rates to 6%, signaling strong lender confidence. Office loans also experienced higher LTVs at 49.5%, but with thinner debt service coverage ratios, suggesting lenders are underwriting more aggressively based on perceived cash flow stabilization rather than a full return of confidence. Other property types, including retail, self-storage, and industrial, faced stricter lending terms with lower LTVs and higher debt yields, requiring more sponsor equity despite easing rates.

BOMA study: Commercial real estate remains a boon to the U.S. economy

REJournals — Mon, 21 Sep 2026 09:51:00 +0000

SUMMARY

Market Research / Economic Impact

A new study by the Building Owners and Managers Association International (BOMA) highlights the significant economic impact of commercial real estate across the U.S. The report, which covers 35.4 billion square feet of commercial property in 79 markets, found that the operation and maintenance of office, retail, and industrial assets generate $609.9 billion in annual economic output and support 3.9 million jobs. The study also notes that for every dollar spent on building operations, $2.22 in total economic output is generated, underscoring the broad network of businesses and workers supported by the sector. Additionally, the research points to the growing influence of data centers on office construction statistics and sustained high levels of industrial construction.

New York State’s Opportunity Zones Deadline Is Fast Approaching

Commercial Observer — Mon, 21 Sep 2026 11:00:01 +0000

SUMMARY

Opinion / Market Commentary

New York State is preparing to announce its updated Opportunity Zone (OZ) designations by September 28, following the permanent renewal and revision of the federal program. State and city officials, including Gov. Kathy Hochul and Mayor Zohran Mamdani, have already implemented policy changes to accelerate affordable housing development, but the revised OZ program is expected to attract additional investment into underserved communities. The new federal guidelines aim to direct capital more effectively to distressed areas by adjusting poverty level requirements and offering greater tax incentives for rural investments, which has reduced the number of eligible tracts nationwide. Developers are keenly awaiting the state's selections, particularly for areas where projects are already in the pipeline, to ensure continuity for investors.
