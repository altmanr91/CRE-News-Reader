Commercial Real Estate Lenders Are Raising the Bar on AI Underwriting

Commercial Observer — Wed, 09 Sep 2026 13:00:30 +0000

SUMMARY

Opinion / Market Commentary

The real estate finance industry is undergoing a significant shift towards greater accountability and auditability in AI-powered underwriting, driven by new governance certifications from MISMO. These certifications establish benchmarks for risk oversight, decision-making influence, and source-data documentation, moving the industry beyond mere operational convenience to institutional compliance. This change will create an "auditability spread" in capital markets, where firms using certified, deterministic software will benefit from faster deal closings and lower transaction friction, while those relying on unverified AI outputs will face increased scrutiny and higher capital costs. The article argues that general-purpose AI tools have largely failed in complex real estate applications, advocating for domain-specific architectures that provide clear audit trails for each step of the underwriting process.

Falling in love with physical retail again? Shoppers seek freedom from the algorithm

REJournals — Wed, 09 Sep 2026 09:55:00 +0000

SUMMARY

Market Research / Retail Report

JLL's latest report, "Resetting the Retail (Algo)Rhythm," indicates a growing consumer preference for physical retail as an antidote to digital exhaustion and algorithmic influence. Shoppers, particularly Gen Z and Millennials, are seeking discovery and a calmer experience that online platforms often lack. While convenience still drives online shopping, brick-and-mortar stores must offer unique atmospheres and curated experiences to avoid overwhelming consumers, as evidenced by 72% of shoppers leaving stores due to clutter or excessive noise. The report highlights that physical retail never truly lost its appeal, with over 80% of sales still occurring in stores, but successful retailers are those who adapt to provide a distinct, engaging, and less overstimulating environment.

Updates to the Wisconsin State Historic Tax Credit Program

REJournals — Wed, 09 Sep 2026 10:02:00 +0000

SUMMARY

CRE Legal / Regulatory

Wisconsin Governor Tony Evers signed Act 238 into law, significantly modifying the Wisconsin State Historic Tax Credit Program to enhance accessibility for historic rehabilitation projects. The new legislation removes the adjusted basis test for minimum expenditure, requiring only a $50,000 spend, which is expected to benefit smaller projects, especially in rural areas. Additionally, projects that previously received credits can now reapply after 15 years, incentivizing long-term investment in historic properties by providing a renewed financing source for subsequent repairs.

DATA POINTS

Federal Historic Tax Credit Program: 20% tax credit against federal income tax liability for qualified rehabilitation expenditures

Wisconsin State Historic Tax Credit Program (prior to Act 238): 20% tax credit against state income tax liability, minimum project expenditure of $50,000 and adjusted basis, $3.5 million maximum credit per project life

Wisconsin State Historic Tax Credit Program (after Act 238): Minimum project expenditure of $50,000 (adjusted basis test removed), $3.5 million maximum credit per project life, reapplication allowed after 15 years
