COPA Passed. Don’t Expect It to Reshape New York City Multifamily.

Commercial Observer — Wed, 28 Oct 2026 12:40:06 +0000

SUMMARY

Opinion / Market Commentary

The recently passed Community Opportunity to Purchase Act (COPA) in New York City is unlikely to significantly reshape the broader multifamily market, despite initial strong reactions. This revised legislation targets a narrow segment of distressed and at-risk buildings, rather than the entire multifamily stock, granting qualified nonprofits a right of first refusal with specific timelines. For many owners of these challenging assets, COPA could offer a structured exit strategy, especially if the program is adequately funded and managed, as these properties have struggled to attract private buyers. While COPA introduces execution risk and potential delays for transactions within its scope, impacting pricing and lender willingness, the overall New York City multifamily market continues to see robust activity, particularly in assets not covered by the new law. The success of COPA hinges on effective implementation, clear rules, and the ability of qualified buyers to secure necessary capital for property improvements and long-term operation.
