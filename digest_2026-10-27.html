Amazon’s Same-Day Delivery Expansion Reignites Demand for Last-Mile Logistics

Commercial Observer — Tue, 27 Oct 2026 13:33:45 +0000

SUMMARY

Opinion / Market Commentary

Amazon's proposed expansion of its same-day delivery network, codenamed Project Mercury, is poised to significantly impact the industrial real estate market, particularly for last-mile logistics. The plan aims to establish over 1,000 new facilities within 10 miles of 80% of U.S. Prime members, making a vast array of products almost instantly available. This move by Amazon, along with similar strategies from major retailers like Walmart, Home Depot, and AutoZone, is driving a decisive shift in retail and industrial real estate, increasing demand for urban infill warehousing and small-bay industrial properties. Experts note that final-mile industrial space currently has the tightest vacancy rates, with structural market changes and growing demand continuing to constrain supply. The imperative for faster delivery is blurring the lines between retail and industrial, as stores are increasingly utilized as staging areas for delivery and pickup, leading to new store designs and investments in distribution hubs.

MARKET INTELLIGENCE

Final mile industrial real estate has the tightest vacancy rates. The market is experiencing a 'flight to speed' in urban industrial, leading to significant rental rate growth for small-bay warehouses and industrial infill portfolios. Retailers are expanding store footprints to accommodate more delivery and pickup options, blurring the lines between retail and industrial real estate. Premiums on rent are observed for retail and grocery-anchored sites designed for delivery, featuring storage lockers, optimized delivery routing, and extra parking for trucks and pickup lanes.
