Bay Area Hotel Market Recovering, But Distress Still Looms

Bisnow News Feed — Mon, 06 Jul 2026 18:03:16 -0400

SUMMARY

Opinion / Market Commentary

The Bay Area hotel market is showing signs of recovery in 2026, leading the nation in Q1 RevPAR growth, but still faces significant challenges from elevated operating costs and lingering distress. Despite increased convention business and events boosting occupancy in some areas, many hotels, particularly economy and mid-class properties, are struggling with reduced overnight stays and higher interest rates on refinanced loans. This has led to a wave of foreclosures and sales at steep discounts, indicating a difficult environment for owners seeking to exit distressed assets. However, major institutional investors like Blackstone are making strategic acquisitions, signaling long-term confidence in the market's eventual full recovery.

MARKET INTELLIGENCE

RevPAR among San Francisco-area hotels is expected to average $142 for 2026, but won’t return to 2019 levels ($169) for at least a few more years, reaching $149 by 2029. Operating costs, particularly labor, insurance, utilities, and supplies, are significantly increasing, outpacing revenue growth and squeezing margins. Hotel loans originated five to seven years ago at low 4% rates are now resetting to high 6% to 7%, nearly doubling carrying costs while net operating income has declined.
