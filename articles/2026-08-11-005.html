New York’s Rent Freeze Was Only An Aftershock. This 2019 Law Was The Quake.

Commercial Observer — Mon, 10 Aug 2026 19:00:45 +0000

SUMMARY

Opinion / Market Commentary

This article argues that New York's Housing Stability and Tenant Protection Act of 2019 (HSTPA) is the primary cause of financial distress for owners of rent-stabilized properties, leading to an increase in vacant, uninhabitable units. The law eliminated landlords' ability to raise rents to market rates after a tenant vacates, making it uneconomical to invest in necessary repairs for units that often require significant upgrades after long-term tenancies. While the recent rent freeze exacerbated the situation, the 2019 law fundamentally altered the financial viability of maintaining these properties, forcing owners to keep units offline rather than incur unrecoverable renovation costs. This situation contributes to a reduction in available housing stock despite some recent gains in the overall number of rent-stabilized units.
