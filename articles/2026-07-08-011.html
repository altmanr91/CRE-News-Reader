Close to Home: How Chicago’s neighborhood retail corridors are outrunning Downtown

REJournals — Wed, 08 Jul 2026 09:57:00 +0000

SUMMARY

Opinion / Market Commentary

Chicago's neighborhood retail corridors are outperforming downtown retail due to a sustained shift in consumer behavior towards local spending and working closer to home. This has led to increased and more consistent foot traffic in areas like Armitage and Southport, making them attractive to retailers, including national brands seeking predictable customer bases. The limited supply of quality retail space in these desirable neighborhood markets, driven by factors like high replacement costs and entrenched ownership, is supporting rent growth and landlord leverage. While downtown areas like the Magnificent Mile are seeing some renewed interest in experiential concepts, their recovery is hampered by the inconsistency of hybrid work schedules, making neighborhood locations more appealing for retailers dependent on steady daily activity.
