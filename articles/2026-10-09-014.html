Momentum building, challenges remain in U.S. office market according to Cushman & Wakefield report

REJournals — Fri, 09 Oct 2026 09:49:00 +0000

SUMMARY

Market Research / Office Report

The U.S. office market is showing consistent signs of stabilization, with the national office vacancy rate declining year-over-year for the third consecutive quarter, according to Cushman & Wakefield's Q3 U.S. Office MarketBeat report. Sublease availability has fallen to its lowest level since 2020, and new construction remains near historic lows. These trends, coupled with continued occupier demand and the removal of obsolete buildings from competitive inventory, are creating a stronger foundation for a tightening office market, despite varying conditions across different markets and asset classes.

DATA POINTS

Q3 Net Absorption: positive 50,000 square feet

Past Year Absorption: positive 15.5 million square feet

National Office Vacancy Rate (Q3): 19.8%

Sublease Inventory (Q3): 89 million square feet

Sublease Inventory as % of Total: 1.6%

New Office Space Delivered (YTD 2026): 7.1 million square feet

Total U.S. Office Inventory Decline (past 6 quarters): 42.5 million square feet (0.8%)

MARKET INTELLIGENCE

National office vacancy declined 25 basis points year-over-year. Vacancy was lower than a year ago in 48 of 92 U.S. markets, including San Francisco, Manhattan, Orange County, Charlotte, Phoenix, and Dallas. Sublease inventory is 33% below its Q1 2024 peak. Vacancy among top-tier office properties has declined 215 basis points over the past two years, while Class-B and -C properties have seen rising vacancy. The national construction pipeline is 23 million square feet. More than one-third of U.S. markets have seen office inventories decline by at least 2% from recent peaks.
