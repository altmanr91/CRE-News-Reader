New York City’s Pied-à-Terre Surcharge Changes the Investment Calculation

Commercial Observer — Fri, 23 Oct 2026 19:52:05 +0000

SUMMARY

Opinion / Market Commentary

A new pied-à-terre surcharge in New York City is significantly altering investment calculations for residential properties, particularly luxury apartments, co-ops, and condos. The surcharge, ranging from 4% to 6.5% of the Department of Finance valuation for the 2026-27 and 2027-28 tax years, applies to second homes and can also impact co-op owners if a neighbor's bill goes unpaid. While commercial office, retail condos, and ordinary Class 2 rental buildings are exempt, some smaller mixed-use Class 1 buildings require individual review. The author suggests that these new costs may make alternative property types, such as rental buildings and commercial condos, more attractive to certain buyers seeking income-generating assets without the surcharge burden.
