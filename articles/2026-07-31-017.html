How Commercial Real Estate is Pricing on Yesterday’s Valuations

Connect CRE — Fri, 31 Jul 2026 05:01:00 +0000

SUMMARY

Opinion / Market Commentary

A recent survey by Urban Land Magazine reveals a unanimous consensus among economists that commercial real estate valuations are not fully reflecting current costs and risks, primarily due to information inefficiency stemming from low transaction volumes. Experts like Michael Acton of AEW Capital Management highlight the challenge of price discovery in a market with limited activity, leading to a misunderstanding of CRE's position in the economic cycle. Conversely, Chad Littell of CoStar Group suggests that investors might be overly focused on present vacancies and interest rates, potentially overlooking future growth opportunities once new supply diminishes. The disparity between the rise in the 10-year Treasury and all-property cap rates, as noted by Kiran Raichura of Capital Economics, further complicates accurate pricing, leaving yield spreads near zero. Josh Scoville of Hines likens current CRE pricing to 'driving while looking in the rearview mirror,' emphasizing the need for investors to consider future replacement costs rather than just present fairness, a sentiment echoed by Joseph Crescio of Manulife Investment Management regarding the judgment-driven assumptions in pricing models.

DATA POINTS

10-year Treasury increase since end of 2021: nearly 300 basis points

All-property cap rate increase since end of 2021: 85 basis points
