The good news in Chicago’s office market? Positive net absorption and plans for a new office tower downtown

REJournals — Wed, 15 Jul 2026 10:12:35 +0000

SUMMARY

Market Research / Office Report

The Chicago office market is showing signs of improvement, with downtown experiencing positive direct net absorption for the first time since 2023 and plans for a new office tower groundbreaking. The suburban market also recorded its strongest first-half net absorption since 2018. Demand for turnkey and high-quality space is a key driver, with move-in-ready suites accounting for a significant portion of leasing activity in both the CBD and suburbs. While overall vacancy remains high, particularly for Class A space, developers are regaining confidence, and the market is on track for positive net absorption by year-end.

MARKET INTELLIGENCE

Downtown Chicago's CBD office market posted direct net absorption of 585,646 square feet in Q2, a significant improvement from -345,914 square feet in Q1. Leasing velocity declined in H1 2026, with 1.2 million square feet of direct deals in Q2 compared to 1.9 million square feet in Q1. Demand for turnkey space is growing, accounting for 36.7% of year-to-date leasing in the CBD (up from 20.6% in 2023) and over 50% in the suburbs. Class A vacancy in the CBD is nearly 22%. Suburban Chicago's office market saw over 2 million square feet leased in H1 2026, with over half a million square feet of net positive absorption. The average new lease size in the suburbs was 27,000 square feet, a 69% increase from H2 2025. Suburban investment sales volume year-to-date is $112 million, with average pricing at $131 per square foot, up from $49 per square foot at year-end 2025.
