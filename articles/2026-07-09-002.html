Feds Pay $1.5B for Two Immigration Detention Centers in California

ACQUISITION

Commercial Observer — Wed, 08 Jul 2026 21:25:49 +0000

SUMMARY

The U.S. Department of Homeland Security (DHS) has acquired two immigration detention centers in California from CoreCivic for a combined total of nearly $1.5 billion. The transaction includes the Otay Mesa Detention Center in Greater San Diego and the California City Detention Facility in Kern County. CoreCivic is expected to continue managing both facilities under existing contracts with U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE). This acquisition represents a strategic shift for DHS, which previously focused on converting warehouse space into detention facilities.

DATA POINTS

Property Type: detention center

Property Name: Otay Mesa Detention Center / California City Detention Facility

Beds: 4,554

Sale Price: $1.47B

Project Notes: The Otay Mesa Detention Center has 1,994 beds and was acquired for $739.2 million. The California City Detention Facility has 2,560 beds and was acquired for $732.6 million. CoreCivic anticipates continuing to manage both centers under contracts with ICE, with the California City contract running through August 2027 and Otay Mesa through December 2029.

COMPANIES/PEOPLE

BUYER: U.S. Department of Homeland Security (DHS)

SELLER: CoreCivic

MARKET INTELLIGENCE

Earlier this year, DHS acquired approximately 7.6 million square feet of warehouse space across eight states for conversion into detention facilities, costing over $1 billion. DHS's Office of Inspector General launched an audit in May to assess the cost-effectiveness of these warehouse acquisitions, and ICE is reportedly looking to sell some of these properties.
