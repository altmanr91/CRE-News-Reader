More States Implement Special Power Rates For Data Centers

Bisnow News Feed — Wed, 08 Jul 2026 17:10:33 -0400

SUMMARY

Infrastructure Investment / Government Policy

Several states are implementing special utility rates and other measures to prevent the surging electricity demand from data centers, particularly those supporting AI, from increasing power costs for residential and commercial customers. This trend is a response to concerns that data center development is creating a supply-demand imbalance in regional grids and could lead to ratepayers subsidizing new infrastructure. Oregon, New Jersey, Alabama, and Delaware have recently approved or are considering legislation to mandate that data centers bear the full cost of grid upgrades and, in some cases, generate their own power supply. These state-level actions follow federal efforts, including a White House pledge and proposed congressional legislation, aimed at protecting consumers from the financial impact of the data center boom.

DATA POINTS

Oregon Data Center Rate Hike: close to 30%

Oregon Residential Customer Rate Decrease: 1.3%

Oregon Commercial Customer Rate Decrease: 2.1%

New Jersey Data Center Power Request Threshold: 50 megawatts or more

Delaware Data Center Self-Supply Mandate: generate or secure own power supply within 10 years
