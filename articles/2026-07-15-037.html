Out with the old, in with the new: SF considers pulling Prop M allocations from inactive developers

The Real Deal — San Francisco — Tue, 14 Jul 2026 19:45:58 +0000

SUMMARY

Infrastructure Investment / Government Policy

San Francisco is moving to revoke Proposition M office allocations from developers who have stalled on commercial projects, aiming to free up valuable development rights and accelerate downtown's recovery. The policy change, outlined by the San Francisco Planning Department Director, would immediately impact projects with little progress and give developers who proposed community benefits up to 18 months to secure building permits before forfeiture. This initiative is part of Mayor Daniel Lurie's broader strategy to prevent developers from indefinitely holding entitlements, especially as the Prop M pipeline has significantly tightened due to a 2020 measure linking office allocations to affordable housing production.

DATA POINTS

Annual office allocation added last year: less than 100,000 square feet

Expected annual office allocation in near future: 100,000 to 200,000 square feet
