Treasuries Hover Around 4.7% As Wall Street Questions If Warsh Is A 'Dove In Hawk's Clothing'

Bisnow News Feed — Fri, 31 Jul 2026 14:28:41 -0400

SUMMARY

Opinion / Market Commentary

Treasury yields are rising again, with the 10-year note flirting with 4.75%, a level many in commercial real estate had considered a ceiling for debt trouble. This increase follows the Federal Open Market Committee's decision to hold interest rates flat for the fifth consecutive meeting, coupled with new Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh's comments that have left investors questioning the Fed's commitment to taming inflation. Analysts suggest Warsh's remarks created uncertainty regarding the Fed's strategy for price stability, leading to concerns about the central bank's credibility. This perceived lack of resolve could further drive up longer-dated Treasury yields, directly impacting borrowing costs for commercial real estate debt, and has already coincided with a significant uptick in CMBS delinquencies.

DATA POINTS

10-year Treasury note yield: 4.75%

CMBS delinquencies (July): 7.86%

Core Personal Consumption Expenditures (PCE) price index (May): 3.4%

Core Personal Consumption Expenditures (PCE) price index (June): 3.3%

FOMC vote to hold interest rates flat: 9-3

Fed inflation target: 2%

MARKET INTELLIGENCE

Yields on 10-year Treasury notes edged up 4 basis points between the Fed's decision and market close on Wednesday, flirting with 4.75% on Friday. CMBS delinquencies ticked up 51 basis points to 7.86% in July, a significant shift from the prior 12 months of stability. The core personal consumption expenditures price index, the Fed’s preferred inflation metric, was 3.4% in May and 3.3% in June.
