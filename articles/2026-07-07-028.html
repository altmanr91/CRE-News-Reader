Chicago homebuyers staring down tight seller’s market

The Real Deal — Chicago — Mon, 06 Jul 2026 16:31:43 +0000

SUMMARY

Opinion / Market Commentary

Chicago's residential real estate market is experiencing a tight seller's market, with median home sale prices exceeding $400,000 and reaching $420,000 in May. The city leads 20 major U.S. cities in home price growth, with a 6.5% annual gain. Brokers anticipate this trend will continue, driven by strong demand and limited inventory, particularly in desirable neighborhoods like Lincoln Park, Lakeview, and Bucktown. Buyers are increasingly waiving contingencies and considering pre-inspections to remain competitive, while sellers are advised to focus on pricing, presentation, and marketing to attract discerning buyers, especially in the North Shore suburbs.

DATA POINTS

Median home sale price (March): $400,000

Median home sale price (May): $420,000

Annual home price gain in Chicago: 6.5%

Annual home price gain in New York: 3.8%

Annual home price gain in Cleveland: 3.2%

MARKET INTELLIGENCE

Chicago's home price growth rate is the strongest among 20 major U.S. cities, according to an S&P report. The market is characterized by a tight seller's market with limited inventory, leading to bidding wars and buyers waiving contingencies. Certain neighborhoods like Lincoln Park, Lakeview, and Bucktown are experiencing frenzied activity, while areas further north like Roscoe Village and Lincoln Square are strong but less intense. The North Shore suburbs also see aggressive competition for well-priced and presented luxury listings.
