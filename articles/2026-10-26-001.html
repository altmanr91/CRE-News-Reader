Querétaro, Mexico’s Data Center Hub, Draws Projects Felt North of the Border

Commercial Observer — Mon, 26 Oct 2026 16:00:19 +0000

SUMMARY

Market Research / Data Center Report

Querétaro, Mexico, is rapidly emerging as a major data center hub, attracting significant investment and positioning itself as a key cloud region for Mexico and potentially the hemisphere. The state benefits from its strategic location, robust connectivity to the U.S., established industrial parks, and a favorable climate that reduces cooling costs. This growth is driven by increasing AI-driven demand for digital infrastructure, with developers finding Mexico a streamlined and cost-effective alternative to the U.S. market, which faces higher labor costs and community opposition.

DATA POINTS

Querétaro's share of Mexico's data center operating capacity: 72%

Total data centers in Mexico (Data Center Map): 66

Data centers in Querétaro (Data Center Map): 24

CloudHQ Querétaro project cost: $6 billion

CloudHQ Querétaro project buildings: 6

First hyperscale data center in the U.S.: 2006 (Google in Oregon)

First data center in Mexico: 2019

Value of stopped/paused data center projects in the U.S. due to opposition: $64 billion

MARKET INTELLIGENCE

Querétaro's geographical position between Mexico City, Monterrey, and Guadalajara provides desirable network latency. The state's mild climate allows data centers to operate with greater energy efficiency, reducing the need for constant chiller operation and mitigating some environmental concerns associated with data center energy and water consumption.
