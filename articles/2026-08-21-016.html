Retail Regains Its Footing as Demand Returns and Supply Stays Soft

Connect CRE — Fri, 21 Aug 2026 05:01:00 +0000

SUMMARY

Market Research / Retail Report

The retail sector experienced a rebound in the second quarter of 2026, driven by renewed tenant demand and consistently low new construction. This dynamic is creating a favorable environment for landlords and investors, despite consumers becoming more selective. Investment sales volume for retail properties increased significantly in the first half of the year, leading to pricing pressure and cap-rate compression. The outlook suggests continued limited supply and modest rent growth, particularly benefiting discount and off-price retailers.

DATA POINTS

First-half 2026 retail sales volume: $33 billion

Year-over-year increase in retail sales volume: 14%

MARKET INTELLIGENCE

Retail demand reversed Q1 negative absorption in Q2 2026. New retail supply remains limited due to higher construction costs and financing challenges, with most new projects being build-to-suit or grocery-anchored developments. Sunbelt markets account for the majority of new construction. Limited supply is supporting modest rent growth nationwide. Stronger sales momentum in H1 2026 is contributing to cap-rate compression. Retail construction is expected to remain sluggish into 2027, keeping vacancies low.
