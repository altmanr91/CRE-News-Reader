A Record Rent, a Demand Reversal and a New Model for the West Side: Chicago industrial at mid-year

REJournals — Tue, 11 Aug 2026 10:05:00 +0000

SUMMARY

Opinion / Market Commentary

Chicago's industrial market achieved a record average asking net rent of over $8 per square foot in Q2 2026, with robust first-half leasing activity totaling 25 million square feet. While the market is healthy, a notable shift has occurred where small-bay infill product has lagged behind big-box absorption, driven by major retailers and third-party logistics firms. Class A industrial properties continue to see rent increases due to tenant demand for quality and functionality, while Class B and C rents have plateaued or decreased. Developers are increasingly focusing on community benefits, such as job creation, green space, and clean energy, to secure entitlements and financing for urban infill projects. The market is also seeing a rise in sale-leaseback transactions as corporate owners seek to unlock capital from appreciated real estate assets, with strong buyer demand compressing single-tenant net lease cap rates.

DATA POINTS

Average asking net industrial rent (Q2 2026): $8+ per square foot

First-half industrial leasing activity (2026): 25 million square feet
