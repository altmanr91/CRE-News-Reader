Data Centers Push Into Frontier Markets

Connect CRE — Thu, 20 Aug 2026 22:00:00 +0000

SUMMARY

Market Research / Data Center Report

JLL's Midyear North America Data Center Report highlights a significant shift in data center development towards 'frontier markets' due to power constraints in established hubs. Developers are exploring alternative power solutions like co-located generation, on-site battery storage, and microgrids, alongside direct utility partnerships, to overcome long interconnection timelines. This push into secondary regions, which account for 77% of capacity under construction, is driven by greater power availability, builder-friendly regulatory environments, ample land, and lower operating costs. While these developments promise substantial economic benefits for local communities, the industry faces increasing public opposition, necessitating greater transparency and community engagement to address concerns and communicate the advantages of local data center projects.

DATA POINTS

Capacity under construction in frontier markets: 77%

Commonwealth Edison project delivery timelines in Chicago: 4-5 years

Commonwealth Edison letter-of-credit commitments: $46,000 per megawatt annually for 10 years

Support for U.S. leadership in artificial intelligence: 79% of respondents

Support for data center development in own communities: 14% of respondents

Estimated local and state tax revenue from projects in states like New York: $100 million to $200 million

MARKET INTELLIGENCE

Grid limitations in Columbus, OH, are pushing users to secondary markets with available capacity, specifically Van Wert, Lima, and Canton. Southern Virginia and the I-95 corridor are attracting data center projects from nearby Loudoun and Prince William counties due to grid constraints.
