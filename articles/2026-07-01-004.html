Rent Guidelines Board Vote Sends New York’s Landlords Into Uncharted Territory

Commercial Observer — Tue, 30 Jun 2026 19:34:52 +0000

SUMMARY

Opinion / Market Commentary

The New York City Rent Guidelines Board (RGB) voted 7-1 to freeze rents for one- and two-year leases on rent-stabilized apartments, a decision that has drawn strong criticism from landlord advocacy groups and legal experts. Critics argue that Mayor Zohran Mamdani's recent appointments to the RGB predetermined the outcome, disregarding statutory requirements for data-driven decisions. Landlord representatives contend that without rent increases, property owners cannot afford to maintain rent-stabilized units, and lawsuits are expected to challenge the legality of the freeze. Conversely, proponents of the freeze cite RGB data indicating a 6.2% increase in net operating income for landlords of rent-stabilized units, suggesting owners can absorb the freeze while tenants face significant financial burdens.
