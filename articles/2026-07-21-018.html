Policy Pro: City rejects buyer protections for pied-à-terre tax, Council seeks rental benefit boost

The Real Deal — New York — Mon, 20 Jul 2026 22:30:00 +0000

SUMMARY

CRE Legal / Regulatory

New York City's Department of Finance has finalized rules for its pied-à-terre tax on luxury second homes, notably omitting an "innocent purchaser" provision that real estate attorneys advocated for. This means buyers could be liable for a previous owner's unpaid tax bill, with the city advising buyers to negotiate risk allocation with sellers. Separately, a City Council bill aims to expand eligibility for the Senior Citizens Rent Increase Exemption (SCRIE) and Disability Rent Increase Exemption (DRIE) programs by raising the income cap to $75,000 from $50,000, retroactively effective to July 1. This initiative seeks to enhance housing security for low-income residents and potentially reduce tenant turnover for landlords, though challenges remain in increasing program awareness and accessibility for eligible households.
