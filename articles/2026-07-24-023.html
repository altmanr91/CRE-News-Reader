Pfizer building incident pits banner project with a conversion pitfall

LOAN

The Real Deal — New York — Thu, 23 Jul 2026 19:00:00 +0000

SUMMARY

A partial stop-work order at MetroLoft Management's conversion of the former Pfizer headquarters in Midtown East is complicating the project's future, as interest continues to accrue on its $720 million construction loan. The incident, involving buckled columns, highlights the inherent challenges and financial risks associated with office-to-residential conversions, particularly the impact of construction delays on project budgets and timelines. Despite the developer's optimism for a swift restart, the need to reconstruct 15 floors is expected to significantly increase costs and extend the timeline for this ambitious 1,600-unit luxury residential conversion.

MARKET INTELLIGENCE

Office-to-residential conversion loans are highly project-specific, with pricing influenced by factors such as the amount of structural work required and building occupancy. The former Pfizer headquarters was vacant, which typically reduces risk and expense, but extensive structural work likely offset some of that benefit. The loan for this project is speculated to have an interest rate of approximately 650 basis points over SOFR, or about 10 percent, though a debt broker suggested it might be lower due to the building's vacancy.
