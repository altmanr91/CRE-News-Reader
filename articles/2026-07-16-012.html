A step back for Des Moines’ office sector?

REJournals — Thu, 16 Jul 2026 09:47:00 +0000

SUMMARY

Market Research / Office Report

The Des Moines office market experienced a challenging second quarter, marked by rising vacancies and negative absorption, particularly in older Class B and C buildings within the central business district. While newer Class A properties saw positive absorption, their higher vacancy rates persist due to large blocks of available space. Suburban markets, however, showed resilience with positive absorption and slightly lower vacancy rates, benefiting from the lack of new construction which is stabilizing overall lease rates despite increased sublease availability.

MARKET INTELLIGENCE

The Des Moines office market is experiencing a flight to quality, with newer, amenity-rich Class A spaces outperforming older Class B and C properties. This trend is consistent with broader national patterns. The absence of new office construction is a key factor in stabilizing lease rates despite rising vacancies. Investment sales are selective, favoring well-located suburban properties and value-add opportunities in the downtown core.
