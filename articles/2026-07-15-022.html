Housing Notes: Developers are in on the joke: Apologize later

The Real Deal — New York — Tue, 14 Jul 2026 14:30:27 +0000

SUMMARY

Opinion / Editorial

This article explores the unique mindset of Manhattan developers, characterized by their relentless pursuit of projects despite obstacles and a tendency to prioritize action over permission. The author, a real estate appraiser and former Columbia MSRED instructor, contrasts this aggressive approach with the more understated style of developers like Nathan Berman, currently undertaking an office-to-residential conversion. The piece uses the infamous "midnight demolition" by Harry Macklowe in 1985 as a prime example of a developer pushing legal and political boundaries to secure project value, ultimately resulting in criminal charges and a significant settlement, though the project itself later faced foreclosure. The author concludes by noting Macklowe's contrasting fondness for humor and warns against developers describing their projects as "art," citing Macklowe's 432 Park Avenue as an example.
