Transwestern: Chicago’s suburban office market is in recovery mode, but that recovery is slow

REJournals — Wed, 05 Aug 2026 10:00:00 +0000

SUMMARY

Market Research / Office Report

A recent report on Chicago's suburban office market indicates a slow but positive recovery, with 106,407 square feet of positive net absorption in the second quarter of 2026. This improvement was primarily driven by limited tenant movement and renewals rather than new leasing activity, as evidenced by a significant renewal by a major pharmaceutical company and a food ingredient supplier. Despite elevated vacancy rates, average asking rents have shown resilience, increasing to $27.16 per square foot. Investment activity is focused on redevelopment and conversion opportunities for obsolete office properties, with minimal new construction observed.

MARKET INTELLIGENCE

Chicago's suburban office market posted 106,407 square feet of positive net absorption in Q2 2026, with all five submarkets recording gains. The direct vacant available rate fell 75 basis points to 18.8%, while the broader availability rate inched up to 25.8%. Average full-service asking rents rose to $27.16 per square foot, an 80 basis point increase year-over-year. The O'Hare submarket had the highest average asking rent at $32.52 per square foot. No suburban office buildings larger than 40,000 square feet were under construction in Q2 2026.
