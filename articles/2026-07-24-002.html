No, the Pfizer Near-Disaster Won’t Slow New York’s Conversion Wave

Commercial Observer — Fri, 24 Jul 2026 10:00:35 +0000

SUMMARY

Opinion / Market Commentary

Despite a structural emergency at the Pfizer building conversion in Midtown, New York City's office-to-residential conversion wave is unlikely to slow. The incident, involving buckling columns at the MetroLoft and David Werner Real Estate Investment project, prompted citywide safety sweeps but is largely considered a 'freak accident' by the industry. While increased scrutiny from lenders and potential new regulations could add to already high construction costs, investor appetite and political will for multifamily housing are expected to sustain the conversion boom. The city's robust conversion market, with 5 million square feet of starts last year and 9.8 million square feet planned, is driven by significant capital investment and the ongoing housing crisis.

MARKET INTELLIGENCE

New York City has the largest conversion market in the country. The city's scaffold law and the 467-m tax exemption (2024) are significant cost pressures for conversion projects.
