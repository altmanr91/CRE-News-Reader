Pfizer building scare tests office-to-resi thesis

The Real Deal — New York — Sat, 11 Jul 2026 12:00:00 +0000

SUMMARY

Opinion / Market Commentary

A structural failure at the former Pfizer headquarters in Midtown Manhattan, currently undergoing an office-to-residential conversion, has highlighted the inherent risks and complexities of adaptive reuse projects. While the incident caused a temporary halt in construction and prompted evacuations, the developer anticipates only a few weeks of delay, maintaining a 2027 completion target for the 1,600-apartment project. This event underscores the challenges of retrofitting decades-old office buildings for residential use, which often involves extensive structural modifications and can introduce unexpected complications, potentially leading to increased engineering reviews, tighter underwriting, and higher costs for projects already facing elevated construction and financing expenses. Despite these challenges, the broader office-to-residential conversion trend in New York City is expected to continue, supported by expanded tax incentives and loosened zoning rules aimed at addressing both high office vacancies and the housing shortage.
