This time, San Francisco’s office boom is flowing south

The Real Deal — San Francisco — Fri, 10 Jul 2026 21:24:27 +0000

SUMMARY

Opinion / Market Commentary

The Bay Area office market is experiencing a unique boom driven by artificial intelligence companies, with San Francisco-based firms increasingly expanding southward into Silicon Valley for spacious office campuses. This trend has significantly reduced Silicon Valley's office vacancy rate and inspired major tenants to renew large leases. Despite high overall vacancy in San Francisco, the demand for premium 'trophy' office spaces remains strong, with some experts anticipating new construction in the city's residential-adjacent neighborhoods. The East Bay, particularly Oakland, has lagged in recovery but is expected to see a shift as the market continues to evolve.

Emeryville average asking rent: $4.28

MARKET INTELLIGENCE

San Francisco's office leasing boom is accelerating, with a notable inversion where AI companies originating in San Francisco are expanding into Silicon Valley. Silicon Valley's office vacancy rate has fallen to 14.1% from 16% last year, and Sunnyvale's vacancy rate dropped from 15.2% to 9.8% year-over-year. Subleasable office space in Silicon Valley is at its lowest since 2016, with 1.3 million square feet available. In San Francisco, rents for premium spaces have increased 40% year-over-year to $140 per square foot, while the citywide average asking rent is $66 per square foot. Class B vacancy in San Francisco fell from 33.4% to 30% year-over-year, and Class C fell from 20.3% to 15.8%. Downtown Oakland's total vacancy increased from 30.3% to 32.3% year-over-year, with Emeryville having the highest average asking rent in the East Bay at $4.28.
