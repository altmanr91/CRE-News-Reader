Crescent Heights snags $332M loan for Chicago’s tallest apartment tower

REFINANCE

The Real Deal — Chicago — Mon, 13 Jul 2026 19:25:08 +0000

SUMMARY

Crescent Heights has secured a $332 million debt package to refinance NEMA Chicago, the city's tallest all-rental apartment tower. The financing, arranged by JLL Capital Markets, includes a $275 million senior loan from New York Life Insurance Company and a $57 million mezzanine loan from PGIM Real Estate. This new fixed-rate debt replaces a previous floating-rate loan, stabilizing the trophy asset amid tightening fundamentals in downtown Chicago's multifamily sector, which has seen strong rent growth and declining vacancy rates.

DATA POINTS

Property Type: multifamily

Address: 1210 South Indiana Avenue, Chicago, IL

Units: 800

Loan Amount: $332.0M

Loan Per Unit: $415,000/unit (calculated)

Notable Features: 76-story tower designed by Rafael Viñoly, includes 70,000 square feet of amenity space.

Project Notes: New debt replaces a $340 million floating-rate loan from KKR Real Estate Finance Trust originated in 2019, which retired an initial $328 million construction loan. Crescent Heights previously explored a sale or recapitalization for the asset in late 2023.

MARKET INTELLIGENCE

Downtown Chicago's multifamily sector led the U.S. in rent growth through the end of 2025 at 5.4 percent. Only 370 units were delivered in 2025 against 1,700 units absorbed, pushing local vacancy down to 5.1 percent.
