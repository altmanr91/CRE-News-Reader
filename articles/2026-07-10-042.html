Office Vacancy Falls In More Than Half Of Major U.S. Cities

Bisnow News Feed — Thu, 09 Jul 2026 12:51:28 -0400

SUMMARY

Market Research / Office Report

National office vacancy rates declined for the second consecutive quarter in Q2 2026, with over half of the 92 major U.S. markets tracked by Cushman & Wakefield experiencing a dip. This broad-based improvement, extending beyond traditional leading markets and trophy assets, suggests a healthier office recovery driven by tenants seeking high-quality spaces and the removal of obsolete inventory through conversions. Tech firms, particularly those in AI, continue to fuel leasing activity, contributing to significant year-over-year vacancy declines in tech-centric hubs like San Francisco and Midtown Manhattan, alongside secondary markets such as Kansas City and Charlotte.

MARKET INTELLIGENCE

National office vacancy rates declined 10 basis points in Q2 2026, marking two consecutive quarters of improvement. Vacancy declined in 49 of 92 markets tracked by Cushman & Wakefield. San Francisco, Orange County, and Midtown Manhattan recorded the largest year-over-year declines in vacancy. Total U.S. office inventory contracted by 33M SF over the past five quarters, with 20 markets seeing at least 1% of their office space removed. Sublease availability declined 15% year-over-year to 96M SF, its lowest point since early 2021. The national average office vacancy rate is 20.1%.
