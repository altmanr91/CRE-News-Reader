“Animal House”: Brickell condo owners outraged by short-term rental goons

The Real Deal — Miami — Thu, 16 Jul 2026 23:00:00 +0000

SUMMARY

Opinion / Market Commentary

This article details the transformation of The Club at Brickell Bay, a 43-story, 643-unit condominium in Miami, into a hub for short-term vacation rentals, leading to significant quality-of-life issues for permanent residents. The building's management and homeowner association board, none of whom reside in the building, are accused of lax enforcement of rules and city ordinances, including Miami's ban on short-term rentals in multifamily residential units. Residents report a range of illicit activities and a decline in living conditions, while investors are drawn to the high profitability of short-term rentals, which can generate up to $50,000 per unit annually. The situation highlights the broader 'Airbnb Effect' where investor profits from short-term rentals clash with residential quality of life and contribute to housing affordability challenges.

MARKET INTELLIGENCE

Miami is ranked among the most popular Airbnb destinations, alongside cities like Las Vegas, Orlando, and Honolulu. Investor websites list Miami as having lenient short-term rental regulations. The 'Airbnb Effect' is a phenomenon driven by wealth migration, rising housing prices, and a surplus of short-term rentals contributing to an affordable housing shortage.
