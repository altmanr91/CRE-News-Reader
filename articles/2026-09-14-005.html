In Los Angeles-Area Retail, a Widening Divide

Commercial Observer — Mon, 14 Sep 2026 10:00:43 +0000

SUMMARY

Opinion / Market Commentary

The Los Angeles retail real estate market is experiencing a significant bifurcation, with high-end and grocery-anchored properties thriving while small businesses, particularly in immigrant communities, struggle. A shortage of long-term retail development has driven demand for prime locations, leading to soaring rents in luxury districts like Rodeo Drive and strong investment in grocery-anchored centers. Conversely, changes in federal immigration policy have negatively impacted foot traffic and sales for many small businesses, especially Latino-owned firms, resulting in substantial revenue losses. Despite an overall retail vacancy rate of 5.83 percent, the market shows a stark contrast between high-performing commercial corridors and struggling neighborhood strip retail and Downtown Los Angeles.

MARKET INTELLIGENCE

Investment sales volume for retail in Los Angeles increased 40 percent in the second quarter, with significant capital focused on grocery-anchored centers and high-quality assets on the outskirts of the metro region. Occupancy in L.A. grocery-anchored centers hit 95 percent in the spring. Rents on Rodeo Drive have soared 50 percent since 2019, with one property commanding nearly $1,400 a square foot. Overall retail vacancy in Los Angeles is 5.83 percent, but neighborhood and strip retail had the highest vacancy at 7.6 percent and recorded 83,000 square feet of negative absorption last quarter.
