Policy Pro: NY electric buildings fight could reach the Supreme Court

The Real Deal — New York — Wed, 01 Jul 2026 23:30:00 +0000

SUMMARY

CRE Legal / Regulatory

A recent ruling by the Second Circuit U.S. Court of Appeals has upheld New York state and city laws restricting fossil fuel appliances in new buildings, allowing the state's 2023 All-Electric Buildings Act and the city's 2021 Local Law 154 to proceed. This decision, however, creates a split among federal appeals courts, as the Ninth Circuit previously preempted a similar gas hookup ban in Berkeley, California. The plaintiffs, represented by Sarah Jorgensen, are considering an appeal, potentially setting the stage for the U.S. Supreme Court to resolve the conflicting interpretations of federal law. The outcome of this legal challenge could significantly influence the pace of electrification in New York's development pipeline and have national implications for building electrification policies.

DATA POINTS

MARKET INTELLIGENCE

New York's power grid is experiencing significant strain due to extreme heat, with electricity demand expected to reach seasonal peaks in the low-30,000-megawatt range. This sustained high demand increases the risk of localized power outages.
