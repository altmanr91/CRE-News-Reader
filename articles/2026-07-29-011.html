A nice surprise: Some good news for the Chicago area’s office market

REJournals — Wed, 29 Jul 2026 10:01:00 +0000

SUMMARY

Market Research / Office Report

Metropolitan Chicago's office market experienced a positive shift in the second quarter, with both downtown and suburban areas recording strong positive net absorption, signaling a potential stabilization in tenant demand. Downtown Chicago saw over 1.08 million square feet of positive absorption, its best performance since early 2021, while the suburban market added another 605,806 square feet. This improvement is attributed to companies leasing more space in higher-quality buildings and a decrease in available sublease space, suggesting a cautious return to office commitments despite a slower economy and hybrid work models.

MARKET INTELLIGENCE

The suburban Chicago office market's vacancy rate fell to 26.1% from 26.6% a year ago. Class-A properties accounted for nearly three-quarters of the positive absorption in the suburbs and 55% of all leasing activity. Downtown Chicago's vacancy rate fell to 24.9% after reaching a record high in Q1 2026. Sublease space in downtown Chicago dropped to its lowest level since the pandemic began. Mid-sized suburban office buildings (20,000-100,000 SF) are outperforming the rest of the sector with consistent leasing activity and below-average vacancy rates. Office investment sales in the suburbs totaled $72.08 million across 11 properties in Q2 2026.
