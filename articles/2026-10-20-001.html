New York Office Tenant Improvement Totals Rise in 2026 Amid Market Shifts

Commercial Observer — Tue, 20 Oct 2026 13:30:42 +0000

SUMMARY

Opinion / Market Commentary

Tenant improvement (TI) allowances in Manhattan's office market have seen a significant increase since 2019, rising 59.2 percent, with a 7.9 percent year-over-year increase to $120.80 per square foot in September 2026, according to Avison Young data. This trend, driven by landlords' need to deliver competitive spaces to attract corporate tenants making long-term commitments at high rents, diverges from the typical pattern where high interest rates would favor free rent concessions over capital-intensive TIs. The increase is particularly pronounced in trophy office assets, where TI allowances rose 15.8 percent to $130.40 per square foot, while Class B and C buildings saw a 4.3 percent decline to $103.40 per square foot, reflecting a K-shaped recovery in the market.

DATA POINTS

Manhattan office TI allowance increase since 2019: 59.2%

Manhattan office TI allowance year-over-year increase (September 2026): 7.9% to $120.80 per square foot

Months of free rent (October 2026): just over 10 months per lease on average

Free rent decrease compared to 2025: 8.6%

Trophy office TI allowance increase (this year): 15.8% to $130.40 per square foot

Class A office TI allowance increase year-over-year: 9.5% to $124.20 per square foot

Class B and C office TI allowance decrease: 4.3% to $103.40 per square foot

Class B and C free rent decrease (this year): 14.3%

Lower Manhattan TI allowance drop: 11.5%

Lower Manhattan free rent drop: 13.8%

MARKET INTELLIGENCE

Manhattan's Midtown submarket has regained its pre-pandemic availability, with Class A office rents reaching a new high. Lower Manhattan has been slower to recover from post-pandemic vacancies, though residential conversions have tightened supply by removing weaker buildings.
