SF leads nation in trimming office vacancy rate as AI drives demand: CBRE

The Real Deal — San Francisco — Wed, 01 Jul 2026 19:00:00 +0000

SUMMARY

Market Research / Office Report

San Francisco's office market is experiencing a significant recovery, primarily driven by the rapid expansion of artificial intelligence companies. The city recorded the largest year-over-year decline in office vacancy among major U.S. cities, falling to 29.7% in Q2 from 34.7% a year prior. AI firms have accounted for 30% of all leasing activity since 2023 and over three-quarters of net absorption, leading to a surge in market fundamentals, including increased asking rents and a record amount of tenant demand.

DATA POINTS

Q2 Office Vacancy Rate: 29.7%

Year-over-year Vacancy Rate Change: -5 percentage points

AI Companies' Share of Leasing Activity (since 2023): 30%

AI Companies' Share of Net Absorption: >75%

Q2 Total Leasing Activity: 2.8 million square feet

Year-to-date Total Leasing Activity: 7 million square feet

Q2 Net Absorption: 646,000 square feet

First Half Year Net Absorption: 2.9 million square feet

Year-over-year Asking Rent Increase: 4.3 percentage points

Current Asking Rent: $71.67 per square foot

Total Tenant Demand: 8.9 million square feet

AI Companies' Share of Tenant Demand: 3.1 million square feet (from 53 companies)

Number of Tenants Seeking >100,000 SF: 30 (11 are AI firms)

Expected San Francisco Office Sales (this year): ~$5 billion

MARKET INTELLIGENCE

San Francisco's office vacancy rate fell by 5 percentage points year-over-year to 29.7% in Q2, the largest improvement nationally. AI companies are driving this recovery, accounting for 30% of leasing activity since 2023 and over 75% of net absorption. Asking rents increased 4.3% year-over-year to $71.67 per square foot. Tenants are currently seeking a record 8.9 million square feet of office space, with 3.1 million square feet from 53 AI companies.
