Across the Line: Why Chicago’s data center demand keeps landing in Indiana

REJournals — Tue, 22 Sep 2026 09:38:00 +0000

SUMMARY

Opinion / Market Commentary

Illinois has lost an estimated $200 billion in data center projects to neighboring states like Indiana and Wisconsin, despite Chicago's strong power generation capacity and green energy attributes. Key factors driving this exodus include the state's Biometric Information Privacy Act (BIPA), which creates uncertainty for AI data handling, ComEd's high deposit requirements and slow power procurement, and a recent pause in Illinois' data center incentive program. As a result, northern Indiana has emerged as a significant data center market, offering cheaper land, comparable tax exemptions, and proximity to Chicago, while industrial land values in core Illinois corridors have doubled due to data center demand.

MARKET INTELLIGENCE

North American data center demand is at record levels, with 25 gigawatts absorbed in the first half of 2026 and 66 gigawatts under construction, 95% of which is precommitted. Vacancy rates have remained below 1% for three consecutive years. Indiana and Ohio are among JLL's top 10 markets for existing and under-construction capacity. Chicago-area data center vacancy is 2.2%, with no contiguous blocks of 5 megawatts or more available this year. Data center developers are exploring rural sites of 500 to 1,000 acres near heavy power infrastructure in areas like Joliet and Minooka, where new generation could take five to six years to come online.
