1 In 10 NYC Cooling Tower Checks Find Legionella-Linked Violations

Bisnow News Feed — Thu, 16 Jul 2026 13:26:46 -0400

SUMMARY

Market Research / Regulatory Compliance

A Bisnow analysis of New York City Health Department data reveals that 70% of cooling tower inspections since 2017 have resulted in violations, with nearly 10% of these being public health hazards directly linked to Legionella bacteria. This widespread non-compliance is complicating the city's response to a recent Legionnaires' disease outbreak in the Upper East Side, where 64 cases have been reported. Experts attribute the high rate of violations to factors like inadequate maintenance programs, failure to test for Legionella, or ignored elevated test results, creating ideal conditions for bacterial growth and dissemination. Despite increased regulations and fines following previous outbreaks, many building owners continue to fall short of compliance, highlighting a systemic issue where self-certification often lacks sufficient third-party oversight.

MARKET INTELLIGENCE

New York City first imposed cooling tower regulations in 2015 after a Legionnaires' outbreak in the South Bronx infected 138 people and killed 16. Regulations were increased in November last year following another outbreak in Harlem that infected 114 and killed seven. Current law requires building owners to test for Legionella every 31 days and clean towers twice a year. Fines for non-compliance have also been raised.
