Industrial firms clear away glut of big-box warehouses

The Real Deal — National — Thu, 16 Jul 2026 18:00:00 +0000

SUMMARY

Market Research / Industrial Report

The U.S. industrial market is experiencing a rapid absorption of large-format warehouses, reversing a pandemic-era glut. This surge in demand, driven by companies seeking to stockpile supplies amid geopolitical tensions and a push for domestic manufacturing, has led to a significant drop in vacancy rates for big-box facilities. As a result, landlords have regained pricing power, with rent escalations for large renewals more than doubling the pace of smaller spaces. Third-party logistics firms and data center support vendors are key drivers of this increased leasing activity, which has reached levels not seen since mid-2022.

DATA POINTS

Vacancy for warehouses 750,000 SF+: 7.3% in Q1 (down from 8.3% a year earlier)

Vacancy for industrial buildings 200,000-500,000 SF: 10.9% (up slightly from prior year)

Total industrial leasing H1: 490 million SF (up 27% from 2025)

Overall U.S. industrial vacancy rate: 8.2% in June (flat year over year)

Third-party logistics firms leased Q1: 30 million SF (20% of all leasing activity, 65% spike from prior year)

Lease escalations for renewals 500,000 SF+: 84% in Q1

Average Class-A lease size Q1: 210,000 SF (highest since 2022, 16.5% higher than 2019)

Average lower-class property lease size: 60,000 SF

MARKET INTELLIGENCE

Vacancy for warehouses 750,000 square feet or bigger fell to 7.3 percent in the first quarter, from 8.3 percent a year earlier. Meanwhile, vacancy for industrial buildings between 200,000 square feet and 500,000 square feet rose to 10.9 percent, up slightly from the prior year. Total industrial leasing hit 490 million square feet in the first six months of the year, up 27 percent from 2025. The overall U.S. vacancy rate was flat year over year at 8.2 percent in June. Lease escalations for renewals on spaces 500,000 square feet or larger averaged 84 percent in the first quarter. The average Class-A lease size, at 210,000 square feet, rose in the first three months of the year to its highest point since 2022, and 16.5 percent higher than in 2019.
