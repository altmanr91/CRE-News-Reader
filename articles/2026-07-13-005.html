Dallas office market continues to stabilize as tenant demand remains positive

REJournals — Mon, 13 Jul 2026 10:06:00 +0000

SUMMARY

Market Research / Office Report

The Dallas office market demonstrated continued stabilization and positive momentum in the first half of 2026, driven by strong tenant demand for high-quality, amenity-rich office spaces, according to a JLL report. While much of the activity involved companies relocating within the metro rather than expanding, this flight-to-quality trend has fueled development in desirable submarkets like Uptown and supported rent growth for premium properties. Despite a selective investment market, Dallas continues to benefit from significant corporate migration, with over 100 companies relocating headquarters to the region since 2018, including a recent major announcement from Samsung. The market is projected to maintain steady performance, with positive absorption and declining vacancy rates through the end of 2026.

DATA POINTS

Year-to-date net absorption (Q2 2026): 500,551 square feet

New office space delivered (Q2 2026): 270,000 square feet

Overall direct asking rents (Q2 2026): $36.91 per square foot

Class A direct asking rents (Q2 2026): $42.86 per square foot

Current vacancy rate: 26.5%

Office space under construction: 1.84 million square feet

Preleased percentage of under construction space: Over 75%

Corporate headquarters relocations to DFW since 2018: More than 100 companies

MARKET INTELLIGENCE

The Dallas office market is characterized by a flight-to-quality trend, with companies favoring newer, high-quality buildings with modern amenities. This has led to rent growth in Trophy office properties and a widening pricing gap between new construction and mid-priced office buildings. The investment market is highly selective, with capital directed towards well-leased, high-performing assets in strong submarkets. Many owners are opting for refinancing or recapitalizations instead of selling at discounted values. Corporate migration continues to be a significant driver, with over 100 companies relocating headquarters to the Dallas-Fort Worth metro area since 2018.
