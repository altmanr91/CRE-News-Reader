SF’s housing frenzy migrates to Marin, Ian Jacobs doubles down downtown, Transamerica gets moving

ACQUISITION

The Real Deal — San Francisco — Sun, 19 Jul 2026 15:00:00 +0000

SUMMARY

The San Francisco housing market's intense competition, marked by homes selling significantly above asking price, is driving a spillover effect into Marin County, which is now experiencing double-digit median price jumps. This trend, fueled by the artificial intelligence boom centered in San Francisco, is a departure from previous tech cycles where Marin's lack of public rail made it less attractive to homebuyers. Meanwhile, in downtown San Francisco, Ian Jacobs, a scion of the Reichmann real estate dynasty, has acquired the four-story retail and office building at 180 Post Street for $48.5 million, continuing his strategy of assembling commercial properties in the Union Square district. Separately, Yoda PLC, the new owner of the Transamerica Pyramid, has signed seven new office leases totaling 113,000 square feet and announced plans for significant renovations to Two Transamerica, an adjacent building, with new amenities expected to be completed by Q4 this year.

MARKET INTELLIGENCE

San Francisco's housing market has seen a 1,700 percent increase in homes sold for at least $1 million over asking price this year. Marin County was the only Bay Area housing market as of July to experience a double-digit median price jump over last year.
