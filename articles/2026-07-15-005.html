Can One Giant Project Change the Narrative Around Downtown Los Angeles?

DEVELOPMENT

Commercial Observer — Tue, 14 Jul 2026 20:15:47 +0000

SUMMARY

The Los Angeles City Council has approved the $2 billion Fourth & Central mixed-use megadevelopment, a 7.6-acre project that will bring 1,589 residences, offices, and retail to downtown. This approval is seen as a crucial signal of optimism for the downtown development market, particularly for housing, despite previous delays and waning institutional sentiment. Industry experts believe the project's green light, following a period of significant property valuation cuts, will encourage further creative development and redevelopment by reducing entitlement risk and demonstrating the city's willingness to approve large-scale density.

MARKET INTELLIGENCE

Downtown Los Angeles land values have seen steep cuts from pre-pandemic levels, with sale comps like Bank of America Plaza and Aon Center trading significantly lower than the neighborhood average of $450 per square foot. The city's Downtown 2040 Plan has increased the portion of downtown allowing by-right development from 30 percent to 60 percent, and the recently approved state law SB 79 increases density bonuses for transit-oriented development, covering a significant portion of downtown. The city's updated Adaptive Reuse Ordinance also offers incentives for redeveloping older buildings.
