Inflationary Concerns Send School Shoppers Back to Discount Stores: Report

Commercial Observer — Thu, 02 Jul 2026 20:21:58 +0000

SUMMARY

Market Research / Retail Report

A new report from JLL indicates that inflationary pressures are significantly impacting back-to-school shopping habits, with parents' budgets increasing by nearly 12% this year to an average of $489 per child. This surge in spending, well above the current 4% inflation rate, is driving consumers towards discount stores and mass merchants. The dollar store sector has entered the top 10 shopping destinations, as families prioritize saving money and the convenience of finding all items in one place. This trend suggests that retail centers offering a mix of value formats and specialty stores are best positioned to attract both high-volume and high-spending customers.

DATA POINTS

Increase in parents' back-to-school budgets (year-over-year): 11.7%

Average back-to-school budget per child: $489

Current U.S. inflation rate: 4%

Parents planning to shop at physical stores: Over 90%

Parents citing saving money as a priority: 68.7%

Parents prioritizing finding full school list in one place: 35.1%

Increase in middle-income families' spending expectations: Roughly 20%

Middle-income families' average spending per child: $495

Walmart popularity increase among surveyed parents: 22.5 percentage points

Walmart popularity among surveyed parents: 77.3%
