In Sun Belt Multifamily, an Uneven Path Back Toward Boom Times

Commercial Observer — Tue, 13 Oct 2026 16:00:38 +0000

SUMMARY

Opinion / Market Commentary

The Sun Belt multifamily market is experiencing an uneven recovery from a pandemic-era construction boom and subsequent oversupply, with different regional markets returning to normal conditions at varying rates. While rent growth surged 30% from 2021 to 2024 and new inventory increased by 673,000 units, the anticipated absorption within two years has not occurred uniformly. Industry experts now project positive rent growth for all major high-supply markets by year-end 2027, a revision from earlier forecasts. The market's complexity is further compounded by recent interest rate hikes, which are altering development and investment calculations. Despite a general slowdown in migration to the Sun Belt, some markets like Phoenix and Las Vegas are still seeing increased domestic inflows, while others like Tampa and Orlando have experienced significant drops. The decline in national multifamily construction starts, particularly in the Sun Belt, signals a rebalancing market, with starts in early 2026 falling to 2016 levels.

MARKET INTELLIGENCE

Yardi Matrix originally expected high-supply markets to return to positive rent growth by the end of 2026, but revised this to year-end 2027. The Federal Reserve's September interest rate hike of a quarter percentage point is impacting developer and investor calculations. Domestic renter mobility fell to 21.6% in 2026, down from 26.7% in 2014. First-quarter 2026 multifamily starts nationwide were 55,000 units, the lowest quarterly level since 2011 and 73% below the early 2022 peak. Sun Belt construction starts in early 2026 were roughly 75% below their 2022 high, and units under construction have fallen to 2016 levels. Sun Belt markets Orlando, Austin, Miami, Nashville, and Phoenix still face a pipeline imbalance, with a further 4% to 5% expected to be added to rental housing stock in certain metros in 2026 and 2027.
