No Vacancy: Tight supply keeps Houston retail humming at mid-year

REJournals — Mon, 10 Aug 2026 10:05:00 +0000

SUMMARY

Opinion / Market Commentary

Houston's retail market is experiencing historically low vacancy rates, driving development to the metro's western edge, particularly in areas like Richmond, Katy, and Cypress. This tight supply is leading to healthy rent levels and encouraging tenants to secure longer-term leases for desirable locations. Landlords are prioritizing financially strong tenants, and there's a notable flight to quality, with demand focused on properties offering visibility, convenient access, and strong demographics. Mixed-use developments are thriving by creating walkable, amenity-rich environments that attract talent and offer diverse retail and dining options.

DATA POINTS

Metro retail vacancy: 5.7%

Statewide retail vacancy: 4.6%

Retail space under construction: 4.2 million square feet

Financing rates secured by DPEG: 100 to 150 basis points below prevailing market levels

MARKET INTELLIGENCE

Houston's retail market is experiencing its lowest vacancy levels since the early 2000s, with metro vacancy at approximately 5.7 percent and statewide at about 4.6 percent. There is roughly 4.2 million square feet of retail space currently under construction across the region. Financing conditions have improved, with some firms securing rates 100 to 150 basis points below prevailing market levels.
