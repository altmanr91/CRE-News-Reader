NY Dirt: City budget ups buildings department, social services spending

The Real Deal — New York — Tue, 07 Jul 2026 11:30:00 +0000

SUMMARY

Infrastructure Investment / Government Policy

New York City's recently adopted budget for fiscal year 2027 includes significant allocations for housing and social services, with a new housing voucher program under the Department of Housing Preservation and Development receiving a $175 million infusion. While HPD's overall appropriation is lower than the previous fiscal year, the Department of Social Services, which oversees the CityFHEPS voucher program, saw a $129 million increase to over $5.7 billion. The budget also boosts the Department of Buildings' appropriation by $30 million to $233 million, with funds directed towards staffing, operations, and sustainability programs. These budgetary adjustments aim to address housing affordability and eligibility gaps through expanded rental assistance programs.

MARKET INTELLIGENCE

Approximately 57,000 rent-stabilized apartments, representing 5.6% of the total supply, were vacant in New York City in 2025, an increase from 3.7% in 2016. This rising vacancy rate is attributed to landlords finding renovation costs for older units exceeding expected rental returns. Tenants' rights groups are advocating for the REST Act to allow localities outside New York City to adopt rent stabilization based on public data like homelessness or eviction rates, rather than costly vacancy studies.
