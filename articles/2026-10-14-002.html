The Plan: The Metropolitan Museum of Art’s New Modern and Contemporary Wing Goes Big

CONSTRUCTION

Commercial Observer — Wed, 14 Oct 2026 10:00:35 +0000

SUMMARY

The Metropolitan Museum of Art has broken ground on the Oscar L. Tang and H.M. Agnes Hsu-Tang Wing, a new modern and contemporary art exhibition space at its 1000 Fifth Avenue location in Manhattan. The $670 million project, funded by private donors, will span 126,000 square feet across five stories and is designed by architect Frida Escobedo. The new wing is expected to open in 2030 and will double the museum's existing exhibition space for 20th- and 21st-century art.

Notable Features: Features a limestone celosía facade, 69,500 square feet of exhibition space, over 18,500 square feet of outdoor terraces, and a 1,000-square-foot cafe. Designed with sustainability and accessibility in mind, including enhanced natural light and reduced solar gain.

Project Notes: The project involves demolition and redevelopment of existing space. The Met is collaborating with the Central Park Conservancy and the New York City Department of Parks and Recreation on outdoor spaces.
