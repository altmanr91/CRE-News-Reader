CRE Strategies Could Help Preserve Value in Life Sciences Mergers & Acquisitions

Connect CRE — Fri, 17 Jul 2026 05:01:00 +0000

SUMMARY

Opinion / Market Commentary

A new CBRE report highlights how corporate real estate (CRE) strategies can help preserve value in life sciences mergers and acquisitions, particularly as pharmaceutical companies acquire sponsor-backed biotech firms. The report identifies "stranding" as a common issue where acquired biotechs, despite smaller footprints, remain only partially integrated, leading to fragmented systems and inconsistent reporting. CBRE estimates that preventable real estate-related risks could amount to approximately 1% of deal value across serial acquisitions, potentially exceeding $100 million over several years for large organizations. To mitigate this, the report recommends focusing on CRE integration rather than immediate consolidation, suggesting a playbook that includes documenting real estate operations within 90 days post-closing and conducting portfolio-wide reviews every 18-24 months to standardize processes and protect long-term value.
