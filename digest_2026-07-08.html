World Cup Delivers Assist For U.S. Hotels, But Not At Super Bowl Scale

Bisnow News Feed — Tue, 07 Jul 2026 16:02:00 -0400

SUMMARY

Market Research / Hospitality Report

The 2026 FIFA World Cup has provided a significant boost to the U.S. hotel industry, though not quite reaching the "Super Bowl-size" economic impact initially projected by tournament organizers. Early returns show strong but not exceptional revenue per available room (RevPAR) and average daily rate (ADR) increases, driven by shorter-term stays rather than the anticipated weeklong visits. Policy Pro: HPD floats 421a rule change to keep more projects alive

The Real Deal — New York — Tue, 07 Jul 2026 23:30:00 +0000

SUMMARY

CRE Legal / Regulatory

The New York City Department of Housing Preservation and Development (HPD) is proposing a rule change to the 421a tax abatement program, allowing developers to amend their affordability plans to qualify for a 2031 construction completion deadline extension. This aims to help projects that initially submitted non-compliant affordability workbooks to switch to eligible options, provided they had previously filed a letter of intent for an alternative. The change is a response to a 2024 housing deal that extended the 421a completion deadline by five years but required deeper affordability commitments. Additionally, the article notes that while the recently approved pied-à-terre tax will lead to new assessment methods for condos and co-ops, it is unlikely to directly spur comprehensive property tax reform in the near future, according to State Senator Brian Kavanagh.

Close to Home: How Chicago’s neighborhood retail corridors are outrunning Downtown

REJournals — Wed, 08 Jul 2026 09:57:00 +0000

SUMMARY

Opinion / Market Commentary

Chicago's neighborhood retail corridors are outperforming downtown retail due to a sustained shift in consumer behavior towards local spending and working closer to home. This has led to increased and more consistent foot traffic in areas like Armitage and Southport, making them attractive to retailers, including national brands seeking predictable customer bases. The limited supply of quality retail space in these desirable neighborhood markets, driven by factors like high replacement costs and entrenched ownership, is supporting rent growth and landlord leverage. While downtown areas like the Magnificent Mile are seeing some renewed interest in experiential concepts, their recovery is hampered by the inconsistency of hybrid work schedules, making neighborhood locations more appealing for retailers dependent on steady daily activity.
