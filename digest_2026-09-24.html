The Power of the Public-Private Partnership for Affordable Housing

Commercial Observer — Thu, 24 Sep 2026 11:00:33 +0000

SUMMARY

Opinion / Market Commentary

This article argues that public-private partnerships are the most effective solution to the affordable housing crisis, which is exacerbated by a national shortage of 4.7 million homes and the loss of 7 million affordable rental homes since 2014. The author contends that traditional public housing authorities face structural constraints and limited budgets, while the private sector, driven by market dynamics, often leads to displacement when affordability agreements expire. The piece advocates for a collaborative approach, leveraging public policy goals with private sector expertise and capital to both build new affordable housing and, critically, preserve existing affordable units through proven financing tools like Low-Income Housing Tax Credits (LIHTCs) and tax-exempt bonds. The author emphasizes that preserving existing affordable housing is an overlooked but essential lever to address the crisis, especially given the challenges and costs associated with new construction.

Households cost-burdened: Nearly a third

Virginia, Maryland Tighten Data Center Rules as Scrutiny Intensifies

Commercial Observer — Wed, 23 Sep 2026 21:10:22 +0000

SUMMARY

Infrastructure Investment / Government Policy

Officials in Virginia and Maryland are implementing stricter regulations on data center development across the Mid-Atlantic region. Maryland's Governor issued an executive order establishing a review process for data centers over 25 megawatts, creating a task force to evaluate projects based on their impact on ratepayers and the grid, and seeking to repeal a sales and use tax exemption. In Virginia, Prince William County has significantly reduced its Data Center Opportunity Zone Overlay District and now requires special-use permits for new proposals outside this area, while the Governor announced a broad "Data Center Accountability Framework" and is proposing legislation to eliminate by-right approval for large data centers and end state subsidies. These actions signal a shift towards increased transparency, environmental oversight, and local control over data center growth.
