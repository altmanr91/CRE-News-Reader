SASBs Dominate Conduits 3 to 1 in 2026 CMBS Market

Commercial Observer — Mon, 26 Oct 2026 12:55:53 +0000

SUMMARY

Market Research / CMBS Report

Single-asset, single-borrower (SASB) deals have dominated the private-label commercial mortgage-backed securities (CMBS) market in 2026, accounting for 76% of issuance through September. This trend reflects a shift in capital allocation, with money rotating away from office and retail properties towards apartments, hotels, and data centers. While conduits continue to finance a broad range of smaller, everyday collateral, SASB deals are increasingly focused on trophy assets and large portfolios, with seven major banks originating the majority of this volume.

DATA POINTS

SASB share of 2026 private-label CMBS issuance (through September): 76%

SASB and large-loan deals total (through September): $66 billion

Conduit deals total (through September): $21.2 billion

Combined CMBS volume (through September): $87.2 billion

Year-over-year combined volume increase: 2%

SASB office volume (2026): $12.5 billion

SASB retail volume (2026): $5.1 billion

SASB multifamily volume (2026): $10 billion

SASB hotel volume (2026): $11.3 billion

SASB data center volume (2026): $6.2 billion

Multifamily share of balance (across both channels): 18%

Retail share of balance (across both channels): 9%

Conduit loans weighted average coupon: 6.43%

Goldman Sachs originated volume: $12 billion

Goldman Sachs SASB volume: $10.1 billion

Citi conduit lending volume: $2.6 billion

MARKET INTELLIGENCE

SASB deals accounted for 76% of 2026 private-label CMBS issuance through September, totaling $66 billion, compared to $21.2 billion for conduits. Combined volume of $87.2 billion is up 2% from the same period in 2025. SASB office volume fell to $12.5 billion from $22.4 billion a year earlier, and SASB retail halved to $5.1 billion. SASB multifamily rose to $10 billion (from $2.9 billion) and hotels to $11.3 billion (from $8.8 billion). Data centers reached $6.2 billion, comprising 9% of the SASB balance. Across both channels, multifamily's share more than doubled to 18% (from 8%), while retail fell to 9% (from 16%).

Opportunity and uncertainty? That’s Pacer.ai’s outlook for the holiday shopping season

REJournals — Mon, 26 Oct 2026 09:35:00 +0000

SUMMARY

Market Research / Retail Trends

Placer.ai's latest holiday retail research indicates that the 2026 holiday shopping season will be characterized by both opportunity and uncertainty, with forecasts for higher sales tempered by economic pressures influencing consumer spending and shopping locations. The analysis highlights a trend towards earlier big-ticket purchases, driven by October promotions, while traditional peak shopping days like Black Friday remain crucial for electronics and home furnishings. For smaller-ticket items, the opportunity shifts closer to Christmas, with affordable gifts expected to appeal to budget-conscious consumers. The report also suggests that shopping centers can capitalize on changing consumer behavior by offering dining, entertainment, and seasonal events to enhance the visitor experience and encourage longer dwell times.

DATA POINTS

Increase in early shopping for electronics stores (2019-2025): 4 percentage points

Increase in early shopping for home furnishings chains (2019-2025): 3.4 percentage points

Electronics stores visit increase (July-September): 0.4%

Top year-over-year visit growth (July-September): Bookstores

Second highest year-over-year visit growth (July-September): Off-price apparel retailers

Third highest year-over-year visit growth (July-September): Hobby, gift and craft stores

MARKET INTELLIGENCE

Between 2019 and 2025, the share of October-through-December visits to electronics stores occurring before Thanksgiving week increased by 4 percentage points, and for home furnishings chains, that share climbed by 3.4 percentage points. From July to September, bookstores recorded the strongest year-over-year visit growth, followed by off-price apparel retailers and hobby, gift and craft stores. Visits to traditional apparel brands, department stores, and home furnishings chains fell year-over-year, while electronics stores saw a modest 0.4% increase.
