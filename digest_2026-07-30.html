For Data Centers, the Case for a Local Balance Sheet

Commercial Observer — Thu, 30 Jul 2026 11:00:16 +0000

SUMMARY

Opinion / Market Commentary

This opinion piece argues for the adoption of a "local balance sheet" framework for data center development to standardize disclosure of project impacts and benefits. The author contends that while data center projects generate vast amounts of information, it remains fragmented across various stakeholders, leading to misunderstandings and disputes, as exemplified by a recent case in Fayette County, GA. The proposed framework would provide a common language for evaluating projects by clearly accounting for benefits like tax revenue and jobs, against costs such as tax incentives and infrastructure demands, enabling better comparison and informed decision-making for developers, investors, regulators, and communities. The author suggests that such a standard could emerge from market expectations, where transparency becomes a competitive advantage, rather than through regulatory mandates.

On the edge of the next growth cycle for industrial real estate?

REJournals — Thu, 30 Jul 2026 10:02:00 +0000

SUMMARY

Market Research / Industrial Outlook

Colliers' Q2 2026 U.S. Industrial Outlook indicates that the national industrial real estate market is nearing the end of its post-construction-boom adjustment, with occupier demand now outpacing new supply for the first time since the construction surge began. This shift, marked by nearly 59 million square feet of net absorption exceeding 53.4 million square feet of new deliveries, suggests the market is effectively working through the speculative development wave of 2023-2024. The national vacancy rate has edged down to 7.3%, and while rental rates show a mixed picture with a slight national decline, the construction pipeline is expanding again, reflecting cautious developer confidence. Colliers anticipates healthy demand will continue through 2026, leading to a more balanced and sustainable market.

Fed Holds Rates In Split Decision As Iran War Hampers Inflation Fight

SUMMARY

Opinion / Market Commentary

The Federal Reserve's Open Market Committee voted to hold its benchmark interest rate steady for the fifth consecutive meeting, a decision influenced by persistently high inflation, a softening jobs market, and geopolitical tensions, particularly the conflict in Iran which has driven up crude oil prices. While markets largely anticipated no change, a significant portion of traders had priced in a potential rate hike, reflecting shifting macroeconomic and geopolitical landscapes that have clouded earlier expectations for rate cuts. Fed Chairman Kevin Warsh emphasized the committee's commitment to achieving its 2% inflation target, noting a "healthy debate" among members on the implications of current inflation, the global backdrop, and available monetary policy tools. The decision to maintain rates allows the Fed more time to assess the long-term trajectory of inflation amidst conflicting economic signals.
