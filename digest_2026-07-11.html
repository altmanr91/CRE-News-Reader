Denver's Population Slowdown Is A Hurdle For Otherwise Stable Industrial Market

Bisnow News Feed — Fri, 10 Jul 2026 14:42:07 -0400

SUMMARY

Opinion / Market Commentary

Denver's industrial market, while stable with an 8.6% vacancy rate, faces a significant hurdle due to slowing population growth, which experts believe is essential for the city to become a top-tier industrial hub. The region's population growth has decelerated, with a 2.5% increase from 2020 to 2024 compared to 6.4% in the prior four-year period, largely attributed to Denver's high cost of living and labor. This slowdown is causing some tenants to seek cheaper operating environments in other markets like Reno, Las Vegas, or Salt Lake City. Despite these challenges, Denver remains an appealing market for investors, with capital partners applying 6% cap rates, though it is perceived to be at a tipping point compared to more rapidly growing industrial markets like Phoenix and Salt Lake City.

Phoenix-Mesa-Chandler metro population increase (2024-2025): 1.1%

MARKET INTELLIGENCE

Denver's industrial market vacancy rate has been stable at around 8.6% for the last three quarters. Net absorption in Denver declined 83% quarter-over-quarter, with the last quarter of negative net absorption occurring in Q1 2010. The Denver-Aurora-Centennial metro area's population increased by 2.5% from 2020 to 2024, a decrease from the 6.4% increase seen from 2016 to 2020. Colorado's annual population growth rate in 2025 was 0.4%, the lowest since 1989. In contrast, the Phoenix metro saw net absorption down 7.7% quarter-over-quarter but up 7.5% year-over-year. The Phoenix-Mesa-Chandler metro's population increased nearly 3.5% from 2020 to 2024 and 1.1% from 2024 to 2025. Investment sales in Denver are seeing capital partners apply 6% cap rates.
