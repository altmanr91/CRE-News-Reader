In Manhattan Investment Sales, the Money Is Moving Faster Than the Market

Commercial Observer — Tue, 29 Sep 2026 13:51:39 +0000

SUMMARY

Opinion / Market Commentary

Manhattan's commercial real estate investment sales market is experiencing a nuanced recovery, with dollar volume increasing but transaction velocity remaining below historical averages. While most property sectors, including office, retail, land, hotels, and free-market apartments, show positive momentum and strong fundamentals, the rent-regulated multifamily sector continues to struggle due to frozen rent increases and rising operating costs. The author argues that while more capital is flowing into the market, the number of properties changing hands has not yet returned to pre-pandemic or long-term average levels, indicating that the 'money is moving faster than the market.' A true recovery, according to the author, will be marked by a significant increase in the number of properties sold, not just larger transaction sizes.

Nine Options: Shallow bay supply runs dry

REJournals — Tue, 29 Sep 2026 09:56:00 +0000

SUMMARY

Opinion / Market Commentary

Chicago's industrial market is experiencing a severe shortage of shallow bay industrial space, particularly for tenants needing 15,000 to 50,000 square feet. This scarcity is driving significant pricing pressure and lease activity in the smaller size ranges, even as overall industrial leasing volume saw a slight dip in Q2 2026. While big box leases contribute to the overall square footage, shallow bay occupiers are responsible for the high deal count, with construction and building materials companies being a primary driver. The limited new construction pipeline for shallow bay facilities, coupled with higher per-square-foot development costs compared to larger bulk warehouses, means that relief for tenants is not imminent, leading to 'sticker shock' at renewal for many.

MARKET INTELLIGENCE

Chicago's industrial market saw 153 leases signed in Q2 2026, with 79 of those between 20,000 and 100,000 square feet, totaling 3.7 million square feet. Overall leasing volume was 10.6 million square feet, down from 14.4 million in Q1 but marking the fourth consecutive quarter above 10 million. Vacancy decreased by 20 basis points to 4.5%, and net absorption reached 5.8 million square feet, significantly higher than 648,700 square feet a year prior. Average asking rent hit $8.12 per square foot. Only 232,600 square feet of speculative shallow bay space broke ground in Q2, bringing the total pipeline to 740,700 square feet across nine buildings.
