How Commercial Real Estate is Pricing on Yesterday’s Valuations

Connect CRE — Fri, 31 Jul 2026 05:01:00 +0000

SUMMARY

Opinion / Market Commentary

A recent survey by Urban Land Magazine reveals a unanimous consensus among economists that commercial real estate valuations are not fully reflecting current costs and risks, primarily due to information inefficiency stemming from low transaction volumes. Experts like Michael Acton of AEW Capital Management highlight the challenge of price discovery in a market with limited activity, leading to a misunderstanding of CRE's position in the economic cycle. Conversely, Chad Littell of CoStar Group suggests that investors might be overly focused on present vacancies and interest rates, potentially overlooking future growth opportunities once new supply diminishes. The disparity between the rise in the 10-year Treasury and all-property cap rates, as noted by Kiran Raichura of Capital Economics, further complicates accurate pricing, leaving yield spreads near zero. Josh Scoville of Hines likens current CRE pricing to 'driving while looking in the rearview mirror,' emphasizing the need for investors to consider future replacement costs rather than just present fairness, a sentiment echoed by Joseph Crescio of Manulife Investment Management regarding the judgment-driven assumptions in pricing models.

All-property cap rate increase since end of 2021: 85 basis points

The Next Office Cycle Will Be Defined by Scarcity, Not Oversupply

Connect CRE — Thu, 30 Jul 2026 22:30:00 +0000

SUMMARY

Opinion / Market Commentary

The office sector is transitioning from a period of oversupply to one of scarcity, driven by a shrinking construction pipeline, improving leasing activity, and a sustained tenant preference for high-quality spaces. This shift is creating a more competitive market where tenants are vying for a limited supply of top-tier office buildings, leading to rising rents and falling vacancies. Opinion / Market Commentary

This article summarizes a Walker & Dunlop webcast where housing industry leaders discussed demographic shifts and their impact on the U.S. housing market. Key takeaways include the country's declining replacement rate, the increasing number of young adults living with parents, and the rise of remote work, all of which are reshaping demand for housing types and locations. While some rapidly growing markets are experiencing a "fallout," more affordable, stable markets with strong job growth are performing well. Empire State Building Observation Deck Hemorrhaging Visitors, Value

Bisnow News Feed — Thu, 30 Jul 2026 14:31:41 -0400

SUMMARY

Market Research / Earnings Report

Empire State Realty Trust (ESRT) recorded a $166 million write-down on the value of its Empire State Building observation deck, leading to a net loss of 15 cents per share in Q2 2026. The observation deck's net operating income (NOI) plummeted by half year-over-year, from $24 million to $12.4 million, primarily due to a significant decline in international tourism and a 45% drop in visitors using discounted sightseeing passes. Despite the World Cup bringing over a million people to the region, ESRT's CEO Tony Malkin noted that the event did not translate into increased observatory visits, as attendees were primarily focused on the sporting event. The REIT is re-evaluating its business model, adjusting advertising strategies, and shifting focus to AI searches to attract more domestic visitors, as competition from newer observation decks like The Edge and Summit One Vanderbilt intensifies.

Philly's Big-Box Warehouse Owners Subdivide Spaces After Developing 'The Wrong-Size Buildings'

Bisnow News Feed — Thu, 30 Jul 2026 14:22:00 -0400

SUMMARY

Opinion / Market Commentary

Following a post-pandemic surge in speculative big-box warehouse construction, Philadelphia industrial owners are now subdividing these large facilities to meet the actual demand from smaller tenants in the city. This pivot to multi-tenant lease-ups is a response to a mismatch between the initially developed "wrong-size buildings" and the market's preference for smaller footprints, which has led to elevated vacancy rates. Developers are incurring additional costs for demising walls, office spaces, and bathrooms, and must navigate challenges like utility splitting, tenant compatibility, and lender approvals. This trend is not unique to Philadelphia, with a majority of recent industrial leases regionally and nationally occurring in subdivided spaces.
