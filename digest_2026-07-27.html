Rise of the Data Center ‘Wildcatters’

Commercial Observer — Mon, 27 Jul 2026 10:30:48 +0000

SUMMARY

Opinion / Market Commentary

A new trend of "wildcatters" is emerging in the data center industry, where entrepreneurs acquire and prepare land for future data center development by securing power access, zoning approvals, and utility connections. These individuals and firms then sell these "powered land" sites to hyperscalers and developers, capitalizing on the immense demand for data center infrastructure. This strategy, likened to oil wildcatting, involves significant capital investment and coordination with utility companies to transform raw land into development-ready sites, often in more rural areas with cheaper land and fewer regulations.

Sunday Summary: Are GLP-1s Affecting Retail? Oh, You Better Believe They Are.

Commercial Observer — Sun, 26 Jul 2026 13:00:51 +0000

SUMMARY

Opinion / Market Commentary

This article explores the transformative impact of GLP-1 weight-loss drugs on the retail sector, noting a significant shift in consumer spending habits. Data indicates an 8-10% reduction in spending at fast-food outlets, coffee shops, and on sweets, leading to closures and bankruptcies among traditional restaurant chains. Conversely, there's a notable increase in traffic to organic markets and gyms, with fitness center membership attrition rates flattening. This trend is directly influencing real estate deals, with wellness and fitness concepts actively seeking space. The article also highlights the success of luxury retailers engaging Gen Z through social media and collaborations, as well as the thriving experiential retail sector, which focuses on providing immersive entertainment and memory-making experiences rather than traditional anchor tenancy models. Additionally, several trophy office properties in Midtown Manhattan have recently hit the market, signaling a potential shift in investment opportunities, while major real estate firms like SL Green Realty and Blackstone reported strong earnings, driven by high occupancy rates and strategic investments in AI-related sectors.

Outdated office buildings in Minneapolis-St. Paul? They’re finding new life as apartments, industrial facilities

REJournals — Mon, 27 Jul 2026 10:06:00 +0000

SUMMARY

Market Research / Office Conversions

A new report from Newmark highlights a significant trend in the Minneapolis-St. Paul market: the conversion and demolition of outdated office buildings. Since 2020, 73 office properties totaling 7.6 million square feet have been removed from the market, with another 570,000 square feet proposed for removal through 2028. This shift, driven by post-pandemic changes in workplace demand, has seen residential development as the leading conversion type, followed by industrial facilities. Government incentives and the suitability of older buildings in downtown areas for residential reuse are fueling this adaptive transformation.

MARKET INTELLIGENCE

The Minneapolis-St. Paul office market has seen 7.6 million square feet removed since 2020, representing nearly 5.8% of the region's office inventory. Residential conversions account for 2.7 million SF of former office space, while industrial conversions total 2.1 million SF. Class B office buildings are the primary targets, with 6 million SF removed, making up 9% of that segment. Corporate campuses have also contributed significantly, with 2.2 million SF removed or converted since 2020.
