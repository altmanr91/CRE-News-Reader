New York Office Tenant Improvement Totals Rise in 2026 Amid Market Shifts

Commercial Observer — Tue, 20 Oct 2026 13:30:42 +0000

SUMMARY

Opinion / Market Commentary

Tenant improvement (TI) allowances in Manhattan's office market have seen a significant increase since 2019, rising 59.2 percent, with a 7.9 percent year-over-year increase to $120.80 per square foot in September 2026, according to Avison Young data. This trend, driven by landlords' need to deliver competitive spaces to attract corporate tenants making long-term commitments at high rents, diverges from the typical pattern where high interest rates would favor free rent concessions over capital-intensive TIs. The increase is particularly pronounced in trophy office assets, where TI allowances rose 15.8 percent to $130.40 per square foot, while Class B and C buildings saw a 4.3 percent decline to $103.40 per square foot, reflecting a K-shaped recovery in the market.

How Bad Would an AI Bust Be for New York’s Office Market?

Commercial Observer — Tue, 20 Oct 2026 10:00:24 +0000

SUMMARY

Opinion / Market Commentary

New York City Comptroller Mark Levine's May 2026 report, "AI and New York City’s Fiscal Future: Planning Scenarios for an Uncertain Economy," highlights the city's significant exposure to both the opportunities and risks of artificial intelligence, particularly given its reliance on the financial industry. The report outlines five potential scenarios for AI's economic impact, with varying probabilities, including a 35% chance of productivity boosts with limited disruption and a 25% chance of AI investment fizzling. Despite these economic uncertainties and broader concerns about AI's societal impact, Manhattan's office market has seen a substantial increase in AI-related leasing, with AI companies accounting for 8.2% of new leasing in the first half of 2026, up from 0.7% in 2020. This growth includes a shift from subleasing to direct deals and a significant increase in average lease terms. While the overall percentage of Manhattan office space occupied by AI companies remains relatively small at around 1%, industry experts like Gabe Marans of Savills and Jeff Gural of GFP Real Estate acknowledge the growing importance of AI tenants, particularly for Class B office owners, suggesting that while a potential AI bust might not cripple the entire market, it could have outsized effects on properties with a high concentration of AI tenants.
