Deloitte: CRE Investors Get More Selective With Capital Heading into 2027

Connect CRE — Fri, 25 Sep 2026 05:01:00 +0000

SUMMARY

Market Research / Outlook Report

Deloitte's 2027 Commercial Real Estate Outlook reveals a conflicted sentiment among CRE executives, with overall business sentiment declining for the second consecutive year despite strong revenue growth expectations. The report suggests that while capital will be available, investors will be more selective, prioritizing asset-by-asset triage, upgrading existing properties, and avoiding sales or conversions to preserve optionality. Logistics, warehousing, and neighborhood retail are favored property types, while hospitality has dropped in ranking. The report also highlights the increasing integration of tax strategy into capital allocation decisions and the rapid adoption of AI, which is outpacing governance frameworks within CRE firms.

JLL Suggests that Energy Efficiency Can Be a CRE Operating-Cost Advantage

Connect CRE — Fri, 25 Sep 2026 05:01:00 +0000

SUMMARY

Opinion / Market Commentary

A recent JLL article highlights that energy efficiency has transitioned from a sustainability effort to a critical component of commercial real estate operating budgets, driven by significant cost savings and increasing regulatory pressures. The report indicates that efficient office buildings can cost 43% to 75% less to operate annually than their least efficient counterparts, translating to savings of $1.58 to $5.13 per square foot. This growing cost differential is exacerbated by electricity prices outpacing inflation in major markets and tightening building performance standards globally. Despite the clear advantages, JLL's research suggests a 'readiness gap,' with a majority of buildings performing poorly against leading standards and CRE teams feeling unprepared, though operational changes and active management can yield substantial savings without major capital investment.

Partial Office-to-Residential Conversions and Financing: The Case for a Floor-by-Floor Approach

Connect CRE — Fri, 25 Sep 2026 13:59:51 +0000

SUMMARY

Opinion / Market Commentary

This article, an opinion piece by Northwind Group's Ran Eliasaf, advocates for partial office-to-residential conversions as a viable strategy for certain office properties. Eliasaf argues that this approach allows owners to preserve the value of strong office components while creating residential value in other parts of the building. He highlights two projects in New York City, 100 Wall Street and 141 Willoughby Street, as successful examples where a floor-by-floor assessment led to a logical division of uses. The key to successful partial conversions, according to Eliasaf, lies in strong residential demand, attractive acquisition basis, supportive zoning, an experienced sponsor, and physical building characteristics that naturally lend themselves to mixed-use, rather than forcing a conversion on unsuitable assets.

SPONSOR PIPELINE

100 Wall Street: Partial office-to-residential conversion, with floors 2-11 becoming 168 apartments and floors 15-29 remaining office space. Northwind Group provided a $219 million construction loan.

141 Willoughby Street: Partial office-to-residential conversion, with floors 8-23 becoming 239 apartments and floors 1-7 remaining commercial space. Northwind Group provided a $208 million construction loan.
