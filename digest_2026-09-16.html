U.S. Apartment Rents Decline for First Time in Eight Months: Report

Commercial Observer — Wed, 16 Sep 2026 14:00:44 +0000

SUMMARY

Market Research / Rent Report

A recent report from CoStar Group's Apartments.com indicates that U.S. apartment rents experienced a slight decline in August, marking the first monthly decrease in eight months. The national average monthly rent fell by 0.03 percent to $1,751, a $1 drop from July. Despite this monthly dip, annual rent growth continues across the country, with the Pacific Northwest and Midwest leading with 2.2 percent year-over-year growth. The report suggests that while elevated supply levels are moderating pricing momentum, the August decline was milder than in previous years, signaling an improvement in pricing conditions.

Spoken For: Renters and investors compete for a Chicago-area multifamily market that can’t build fast enough

REJournals — Wed, 16 Sep 2026 09:42:00 +0000

SUMMARY

Opinion / Market Commentary

The Chicago-area multifamily market is experiencing intense competition from both renters and investors due to a significant supply-demand imbalance. Demand for apartments continues to climb while new supply, particularly in the suburbs, lags far behind population and household formation trends. This has led to high occupancy rates and strong rent growth across various submarkets, with both urban and suburban properties seeing units lease quickly with minimal concessions. The lack of new construction is attributed to high development costs, insurance expenses, elevated interest rates, and lengthy entitlement processes, pushing developers towards less dense projects and conversions.

Rent growth on turnover units (Class B and C assets): 4% to 7%
