Private-Label CMBS Accounts for Half of Delinquencies in Securitized Multifamily Loans

Connect CRE — Thu, 22 Oct 2026 02:03:43 +0000

SUMMARY

Market Research / Delinquency Report

A recent report by Trepp highlights that private-label CMBS now accounts for half of all delinquent securitized multifamily loans, despite representing only 12% of the total outstanding balance. This marks a significant shift from two years prior when CRE CLOs held the largest share of delinquent multifamily debt. The high delinquency rate in private-label CMBS, at 7.69%, contrasts sharply with Freddie Mac's 0.80% delinquency rate, suggesting a concentration of distress within the private-label segment. The report also notes that CRE CLO managers' ability to remove credit-impaired loans from pools might understate the true level of distress in that sector.

Four Signals From the New York City Market That Matter More Than a Headline

Commercial Observer — Thu, 22 Oct 2026 11:00:21 +0000

SUMMARY

Opinion / Market Commentary

This opinion piece argues that the New York City residential real estate market is characterized by strategic, individual decisions rather than broad, uniform responses to market conditions. The author identifies four key patterns: sellers are increasingly opting to rent between selling and buying to maintain flexibility; investors are selectively acquiring properties with strong rental appeal, focusing on long-term value; desirable homes can still command above-asking prices when they meet specific buyer needs; and off-market transactions are becoming a more deliberate path, often facilitated by agent relationships and social media. The author concludes that understanding the market requires observing these intentional decisions rather than relying on single metrics or forecasts.

Gaining strength: Milwaukee industrial market benefitting from strong demand for manufacturing space

REJournals — Thu, 22 Oct 2026 09:53:00 +0000

SUMMARY

Market Research / Industrial Report

Milwaukee's industrial real estate market is experiencing a resurgence, primarily driven by robust demand for manufacturing facilities, according to a recent report. This increased demand has led to a significant improvement in occupancy rates and a notable decline in overall industrial vacancy, marking the first substantial drop since 2022. Despite challenges posed by trade policy uncertainty, a restrained development pipeline is expected to further support property performance and contribute to continued tightening of market conditions through the end of the year.

DATA POINTS

Manufacturing employment share (Milwaukee metro): 13%

Metro-wide industrial vacancy decline (12 months ended June 2026): 90 basis points

Industrial space forecast for completion in 2026: 1.65 million square feet

Inventory increase forecast for 2026: 0.6%

Industrial space under construction (as of June): Less than 1 million square feet

Waukesha County industrial vacancy: 2.4%

Racine County industrial vacancy (June): 7.9%

Average asking rent decline (first half 2026): 2%

Average asking rent (first half 2026): $5.98 per square foot

Full-year forecast average asking rent: $6.16 per square foot

Industrial investment deal flow increase (year ended June): Nearly 50%

Average sale price for properties near airport (12 months ended June): $73 per square foot

MARKET INTELLIGENCE

Manufacturing payrolls in the Milwaukee metro area edged higher during the first eight months of 2026 after declining steadily since 2023. The market recorded a decade-high level of manufacturing leasing, with occupancy gains across different property sizes. Older industrial buildings in established central Milwaukee County locations showed strong absorption. Warehouse market net absorption returned to 2022-2024 levels, supported by manufacturing-related storage and data-center development. Distribution properties, however, experienced negative net absorption for a third consecutive year. Approximately 1.65 million square feet of industrial space is forecast for completion in 2026, representing a 0.6% inventory increase, which is more than 40% below the prior 10-year average. Manufacturing space accounts for roughly 60% of projected completions. As of June, less than 1 million square feet was under construction, the lowest level since 2021. Nearly all submarkets recorded lower vacancy, with Ozaukee, Milwaukee, and Washington counties posting declines over 100 basis points. Waukesha County had the tightest industrial market at 2.4% vacancy. Average asking rents declined 2% in the first half of 2026 to $5.98 per square foot, with a full-year forecast of $6.16 per square foot. Industrial investment deal flow rose nearly 50%, with manufacturing property transactions reaching a multiyear high. Owner-users were net sellers for a sixth consecutive year, but acquisitions accelerated sharply in the first half of 2026, often involving pre-1990 properties. Milwaukee Mitchell International Airport plans a 333,000-square-foot South Air Cargo Facility, potentially quadrupling cargo capacity.
