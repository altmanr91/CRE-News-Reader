Net Lease Cap Rates Tick Upward in Q2 2026

Connect CRE — Mon, 13 Jul 2026 09:55:56 +0000

SUMMARY

Market Research / Net Lease Report

Overall single-tenant net lease cap rates saw a slight increase in the second quarter of 2026, rising two basis points to 6.82%, according to The Boulder Group. This uptick was primarily driven by retail and industrial sectors, which experienced increases of five and ten basis points respectively, while office cap rates held steady. The shift is attributed to the Federal Reserve's revised interest rate projections, which removed an anticipated rate cut and introduced the possibility of a hike later in the year, influencing investor calculations for the latter half of 2026. Despite these changes, transaction volume is expected to remain stable, with continued investor interest in the fundamental appeal of net lease assets, potentially bolstered by corporate sale-leaseback activity.

Chicago's Older Apartments Are Running Out Of Room To Raise Rent

Bisnow News Feed — Sun, 12 Jul 2026 13:00:17 -0400

SUMMARY

Opinion / Market Commentary

Chicago's Class-C apartment market is facing significant challenges despite overall multifamily tailwinds, as rising operating costs for insurance, property taxes, and utilities are outpacing rent growth for older properties. This squeeze on margins is particularly acute for vintage stock, where some investors who focused on cosmetic upgrades in recent years deferred vital maintenance, making these properties harder to sell. While rent-by-necessity rates have increased substantially, they are now approaching affordability limits for workforce tenants, compressing the runway for further rent hikes. The market is seeing a return to more experienced operators who are selectively pursuing value-add opportunities, often with below-market rents or assumable low-rate debt, as new buyers face higher financing costs.

Dallas office market continues to stabilize as tenant demand remains positive

REJournals — Mon, 13 Jul 2026 10:06:00 +0000

SUMMARY

Market Research / Office Report

The Dallas office market demonstrated continued stabilization and positive momentum in the first half of 2026, driven by strong tenant demand for high-quality, amenity-rich office spaces, according to a JLL report. While much of the activity involved companies relocating within the metro rather than expanding, this flight-to-quality trend has fueled development in desirable submarkets like Uptown and supported rent growth for premium properties. Despite a selective investment market, Dallas continues to benefit from significant corporate migration, with over 100 companies relocating headquarters to the region since 2018, including a recent major announcement from Samsung. The market is projected to maintain steady performance, with positive absorption and declining vacancy rates through the end of 2026.

MARKET INTELLIGENCE

The Dallas office market is characterized by a flight-to-quality trend, with companies favoring newer, high-quality buildings with modern amenities. This has led to rent growth in Trophy office properties and a widening pricing gap between new construction and mid-priced office buildings. The investment market is highly selective, with capital directed towards well-leased, high-performing assets in strong submarkets. Many owners are opting for refinancing or recapitalizations instead of selling at discounted values. Corporate migration continues to be a significant driver, with over 100 companies relocating headquarters to the Dallas-Fort Worth metro area since 2018.

Market Research / Multi-Market Report
