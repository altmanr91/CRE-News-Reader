Why Job Numbers Don’t Tell the Whole Story

Connect CRE — Fri, 18 Sep 2026 05:01:00 +0000

SUMMARY

Opinion / Market Commentary

This article argues that headline job numbers alone do not provide a complete picture of the U.S. labor market, which is sending mixed signals. While August saw 162,000 jobs added, primarily in food services, local government, and healthcare, hiring remains sluggish, and small businesses report difficulty filling openings. The author highlights discrepancies such as declining employment among younger workers, particularly in AI-exposed occupations, and a persistent skills mismatch in manufacturing. For commercial real estate, this means that investment and development decisions should consider employment types, specific labor needs, and employer-attractive locations rather than just national job growth figures. The article concludes that a nuanced understanding of industry, occupation, worker age, company strategy, and geography is crucial for predicting demand across various property types.

Transparency Is Becoming a Prerequisite for CRE Capital

Connect CRE — Fri, 18 Sep 2026 05:01:00 +0000

SUMMARY

Market Research / Transparency Report

Transparency in commercial real estate has evolved from a measure of market maturity to a critical prerequisite for capital deployment, according to a new report by JLL. The "Global Real Estate Transparency Index, 2026" highlights that transaction volumes in the most transparent markets have significantly outpaced less transparent ones, attracting over 98% of global real estate capital. This increased transparency facilitates earlier price discovery and enables investors to better understand market dynamics, price assets, and deploy capital more efficiently, even in alternative sectors where data can be limited. The report also emphasizes the need for strengthened valuation methodologies, standardized reporting, and clearer communication of liquidity terms to support a broader investor base and help all market participants navigate risk and opportunity.

Construction’s Growth Story Depends on What’s Being Built

Connect CRE — Fri, 18 Sep 2026 05:01:00 +0000

SUMMARY

Opinion / Market Commentary

The U.S. construction market is experiencing a bifurcated growth story, with overall headline numbers masking significant disparities across sectors. While new construction starts saw a 20% month-over-month decline in June after a 33.5% gain in May, this volatility is largely driven by a few massive projects in healthcare, manufacturing, utilities, and particularly data centers. Data centers and energy-related projects are primarily responsible for current construction growth, creating uneven backlogs for contractors, while residential and institutional sectors remain weak. The demand for design services, as indicated by the American Institute of Architects' Billings Index, suggests that broader nonresidential construction activity will likely remain soft into early 2027, highly sensitive to interest rate cuts and trade policy. Despite near-record employment levels, labor demand is concentrated in these high-growth segments, reinforcing the uneven distribution of activity across the industry.

Forget Property Deeds: The Moon’s Real Estate Could Run on Operating Rights

Connect CRE — Thu, 17 Sep 2026 22:30:00 +0000

SUMMARY

Opinion / Market Commentary

A Deloitte report, "Building the Lunar Economy," suggests that future commercial real estate on the Moon will operate under a system of operating rights and access rather than traditional property deeds due to the Outer Space Treaty. The report, informed by insights from industry leaders, highlights the eroding barriers to entry and growing promise of lunar resources, particularly at the South Pole, which is targeted by upcoming missions like NASA's Artemis Base Camp and China's International Lunar Research Station. Key infrastructure in transportation, power, communications, construction, and life support will be necessary to support commercial activity, with governments likely serving as initial anchor customers. While significant engineering and commercial viability challenges remain, the report emphasizes that the focus is shifting from whether humanity will return to the Moon to what happens once a sustained presence is established.
