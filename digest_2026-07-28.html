Commercial Observer — Mon, 27 Jul 2026 19:56:06 +0000

SUMMARY

Opinion / Market Commentary

This article argues that the future of office leasing will be shaped by where AI companies choose to invest and build, rather than by general return-to-office policies. The author, a real estate software executive, highlights a significant concentration of AI office demand in specific submarkets of San Francisco, San Jose, and New York City, which collectively account for nearly three-quarters of current AI square footage. The author advises CRE executives to use forward-looking indicators of AI demand to inform capital allocation and asset positioning, emphasizing the importance of internal AI capability and data infrastructure for long-term advantage.

'WeWork 2.0': Booming AI Startups Concern Some Data Center Execs

Bisnow News Feed — Mon, 27 Jul 2026 12:36:11 -0400

SUMMARY

Opinion / Market Commentary

The rapid growth of AI-focused computing providers, known as neoclouds, is driving significant demand in the data center market, but some industry executives express skepticism about their business models. These startups often lease data center capacity long-term while offering short-term computing power to customers, creating a potential mismatch that raises concerns about their long-term sustainability, reminiscent of WeWork's challenges. This dynamic complicates financing for data center developments, which traditionally rely on investment-grade tenants. While new financing structures and a class of risk-tolerant investors are emerging, industry leaders caution that thorough due diligence is essential to mitigate the inherent uncertainties of this nascent sector.

Deep Pockets, Sharper Pencils: Capital crowds into Chicago industrial

REJournals — Tue, 28 Jul 2026 10:07:00 +0000

SUMMARY

Opinion / Market Commentary

Chicago's industrial capital markets are experiencing robust activity, with significant capital inflows driving strong pricing for high-quality, infill assets, particularly in the small-bay, multi-tenant segment. Lenders are actively competing for new business, leading to aggressive bidding and looser structures, as evidenced by a proprietary credit index reaching an all-time high. Despite rising interest rates, industrial cap rates have remained stable or even tightened for Class A infill properties, a trend attributed to strong market fundamentals offsetting rate impacts. Investors are increasingly focused on infill industrial properties with historically low vacancy rates and limited new supply, though underwriting these more complex assets requires specialized expertise.

MARKET INTELLIGENCE

Stabilized, well-located Chicago industrial product is trading in the mid-5% to low-6% cap rate range. Demand is greatest for true infill industrial, particularly mid-bay buildings of 75,000 to 300,000 square feet, where vacancy rates are historically low and development options are constrained.
