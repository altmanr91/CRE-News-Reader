CRE CLO Distress Accelerates in August

Commercial Observer — Tue, 08 Sep 2026 13:24:00 +0000

SUMMARY

Market Research / Distress Report

The distress rate for commercial real estate collateralized loan obligations (CRE CLOs) surged to 28% in August from 19% in July, marking the most significant one-month increase this year across all deal types. This acceleration, along with a stable 22% distress rate for single-asset, single-borrower (SASB) commercial mortgage-backed securities, is primarily driven by 2021 and 2022 vintage loans. The distress is concentrated in a few large deals, particularly in multifamily collateral for CRE CLOs in the Sun Belt and office/lab properties for SASBs in major coastal markets, reflecting challenges with refinancing and unmet rent growth projections.

MARKET INTELLIGENCE

Distress rates for conduit, Freddie Mac, and single-family rental loans have remained under 5% for eight months, indicating that the current double-digit distress is specific to CRE CLO and SASB categories, particularly for 2021 and 2022 vintage collateral. Office and mixed-use loans maturing in the next nine months face a refinancing gap of 170 to 180 basis points above their in-place notes, which is the widest gap among all property types.

Office-Leasing Bidding Wars in Manhattan Are Now a Thing

Commercial Observer — Tue, 08 Sep 2026 13:00:36 +0000

SUMMARY

Opinion / Market Commentary

Manhattan's office market is experiencing a surge in demand for high-quality, built-out office spaces, particularly in desirable submarkets, leading to bidding wars among prospective tenants. This intense competition is fueled by a combination of the return-to-office trend, residential conversions reducing supply, and a significant boom in AI-driven tech companies that require immediate, move-in ready spaces due to rapid growth. The scarcity of such spaces has led to accelerated transaction timelines and increased asking rents, with some properties receiving offers significantly above initial asking prices, a dynamic not seen in decades.

DATA POINTS

Manhattan total available office supply (August): 65.4 million square feet

Midtown average asking rent: $84.68 per square foot

Midtown South share of August office demand: 47 percent

MARKET INTELLIGENCE

Manhattan's total available office supply fell to 65.4 million square feet in August, the lowest since September 2020. Sublet supply is at 2019 lows. Midtown's availability is at its lowest since March 2020, with an average asking rent of $84.68 per square foot. Downtown asking rents have climbed for nine straight months. If current demand continues, Manhattan's annual leasing volume could be the highest since 2000.
