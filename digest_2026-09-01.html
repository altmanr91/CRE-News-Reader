Forbearances, Modifications Carry Weight in Latest Data

Commercial Observer — Tue, 01 Sep 2026 13:57:56 +0000

SUMMARY

Market Research / Loan Modifications

CRED iQ's latest analysis of commercial mortgage-backed securities (CMBS) and commercial real estate collateralized loan obligation (CRE CLO) loan modifications from May through July 2026 reveals a significant shift in the landscape of distressed commercial real estate. The report tracked 82 modified loans totaling $2.36 billion, noting that while maturity extensions remain a key tool, forbearances and combination modifications are now playing a more prominent role, indicating lenders are employing a broader range of strategies beyond simple 'extend and pretend' tactics. Notably, multifamily loans have emerged as the leading property type for modifications, surpassing hotels and office properties, suggesting that rate resets on floating-rate loans and slower rent growth are impacting borrowers in this sector. The data also indicates that distress is now concentrated in midsize loans, particularly those in the $20 million to $50 million range, rather than a few mega-loans, reflecting a broadening of modification activity across the market.

Trepp’s Stephen Buschbom and Andy Boettcher On a Very Busy 2026 for CMBS

Commercial Observer — Tue, 01 Sep 2026 13:00:10 +0000

SUMMARY

Opinion / Market Commentary

The commercial mortgage-backed securities (CMBS) market is poised to surpass its initial issuance projections for 2026, driven primarily by single-asset, single-borrower (SASB) transactions. Trepp analysts Stephen Buschbom and Andy Boettcher note that while overall CMBS volume is strong, the market is experiencing a significant shift in composition, with SASB deals accounting for a dominant share compared to pre-COVID levels. This trend is attributed to buyer preferences for more targeted portfolio management and banks' increased comfort in managing specific credit risks. Despite rising distress, particularly in the office sector, the market is showing signs of stabilization, with banks having largely reserved for their commercial real estate exposure and demonstrating healthy lending growth, which in turn supports liquidity for CMBS maturities and rollovers.
