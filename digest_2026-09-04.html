Amid Higher-for-Longer Mortgage Rates, Multifamily Pros Find a Silver Lining

Commercial Observer — Fri, 04 Sep 2026 10:43:03 +0000

SUMMARY

Opinion / Market Commentary

This article analyzes how persistently high mortgage rates, which have remained elevated since late 2022 and spiked to nearly 8% in late 2023, are creating a silver lining for the multifamily and single-family rental sectors. With homeownership becoming significantly more expensive than renting, demand for rental housing is surging as households are priced out of buying. This shift, coupled with a contracting pipeline for new home construction, is expected to drive healthy rent growth and increased deal flow for multifamily assets, which also benefit from shorter lease terms allowing for quicker repricing in an inflationary environment.

World Cup games kicked retail, restaurant sales up … but mostly for retailers with the right strategies and location

REJournals — Fri, 04 Sep 2026 10:09:00 +0000

SUMMARY

Market Research / Retail & Dining Patterns

A new report from Colliers and Placer.ai analyzes retail and dining patterns during the FIFA 2025 World Cup, concluding that proximity to venues alone did not guarantee a business boost. The study, "7 World Cup Lessons for Retail, Dining & Real Estate," found that the economic impact was highly localized, with the strongest gains concentrated around stadiums, Fan Fests, and commercial districts that actively integrated into the fan experience. Properties that performed best became part of the fan experience through strategic tenant mix, gathering spaces, or activations, rather than just relying on increased traffic. This suggests that for future mega-events, intentional connections between events and commercial districts are crucial for landlords and investors to maximize consumer spending.

Portland Industrial Market Finds Stability as Supply Pressures Ease

REBusinessOnline — Fri, 04 Sep 2026 11:34:00 +0000

SUMMARY

Opinion / Market Commentary

Portland's industrial market is transitioning into a more balanced phase, characterized by stabilizing port activity and a significant slowdown in new construction. This moderation in the development pipeline is expected to help the market absorb existing inventory and ease competitive pressures, despite elevated vacancy rates. Demand remains robust for modern warehouse and distribution facilities, particularly those under 50,000 square feet and located near major transportation corridors, driven by e-commerce, local manufacturing, and service businesses. Investment activity is anticipated to focus on well-located assets with strong logistics advantages and properties supporting advanced manufacturing, signaling a foundation for measured, long-term growth.
