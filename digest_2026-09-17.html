Warehub Founder James Holbrook On Industrial’s Flexibility Needs

Commercial Observer — Thu, 17 Sep 2026 11:05:18 +0000

SUMMARY

Opinion / Market Commentary

James Holbrook, founder of Warehub, discusses the evolving landscape of U.S. industrial real estate, highlighting a shift from long-term commitments to a greater demand for flexibility among logistics users. He explains that Warehub's platform standardizes short-term industrial leases, allowing landlords to monetize vacant space and tenants to secure temporary distribution capacity. Holbrook notes that this trend is driven by factors like e-commerce acceleration, tariff instability, and seasonal compression, which necessitate more adaptive geographic placement of infrastructure to reduce transportation costs and improve delivery times. He emphasizes that flexible warehousing helps tenants create regional distribution nodes to manage inventory surges and stabilize logistics expenses, addressing the current market's vacancy challenges by making existing building stock more efficient.

Colliers’ Industrial Tenant Tracker: Expansion plans back on the menu

REJournals — Thu, 17 Sep 2026 09:03:00 +0000

SUMMARY

Market Research / Industrial Report

The U.S. industrial market is experiencing a robust rebound in the first half of 2026, with new bulk industrial occupancies of 100,000 square feet or more increasing by 25% year-over-year to 221 million square feet. Net absorption surged by 82% to 108 million square feet, indicating that tenants are actively resuming expansion plans and absorbing available space at a faster pace. This growth is driven by a broadening base of occupiers, including third-party logistics providers, manufacturing, building materials, and data center infrastructure companies, with e-commerce users also showing renewed activity. The market is poised for a new growth cycle as demand outpaces new supply, leading to an expected gradual decline in vacancy rates through 2027.

MARKET INTELLIGENCE

The industrial market has moved beyond the post-pandemic slowdown, with tenants resuming expansion plans and absorbing available space at a faster pace. Users moved into 26 buildings of 1M SF or larger in the first half of the year, nearly double the 15 new occupancies during the same period last year. More than one-third of these move-ins were build-to-suit facilities or user purchases, with activity concentrated in the West, Southeast, and Southcentral regions. The Midwest recorded the most bulk move-ins during the first half of 2026, with 196 occupancies totaling 49M SF, an 18% increase from the same period last year. The West led in volume, with 184 occupancies totaling 54M SF, up 14% year over year. Growth was strongest in the Northeast, where occupancy volume surged 173% to 25M SF across 86 move-ins. The Southeast was the only region to record a decline, with volume falling 17% to 44M SF across 145 occupancies. Big-box vacancy rates have declined for several consecutive quarters nationally and across most major markets. The construction pipeline remains well below its recent peak and pre-COVID levels.
