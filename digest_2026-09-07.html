Power Is No Longer an Input in Data Centers. It Is the Product.

Commercial Observer — Mon, 07 Sep 2026 13:00:09 +0000

SUMMARY

Opinion / Market Commentary

This article argues that power availability has become the primary determinant of value and site selection for data center development, rather than a mere input. The author contends that traditional real estate factors like land and fiber are secondary to the ability to deliver electricity when customers need it, especially for AI companies with rapid deployment timelines. This shift is forcing developers to engage more deeply with energy infrastructure, seeking out substations, transmission access, and gas connections, and re-evaluating sites based on existing electrical capacity and phased energization potential. The article concludes that 'digital dirt'—land with inherited, difficult-to-replicate infrastructure—is gaining new relevance, exemplified by former industrial sites like Paducah, Kentucky, which offer significant power and water infrastructure for AI data centers.

'We Aren't Competitive': Power, Taxes Keep Colorado On The Data Center Sidelines

Bisnow Latest News Feed — Sun, 06 Sep 2026 15:38:01 -0400

SUMMARY

Opinion / Market Commentary

Colorado is largely missing out on the artificial intelligence data center boom, a situation attributed by industry leaders to the state's tax structure and power supply challenges. Despite being home to several major data center companies, Colorado's high energy costs and unfavorable tax policies make it less competitive compared to other states that offer more incentives and cheaper power for these energy-intensive facilities. This has led to a significant portion of new data center development bypassing the state, impacting its potential for economic growth in the tech sector.
