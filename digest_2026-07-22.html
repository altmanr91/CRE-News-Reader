Infrastructure Investment / Government Policy

The second iteration of the Opportunity Zone program, established by the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, is nearing its final mapping phase, with governors preparing to submit their proposed census tracts to the Treasury Department by September 28. This new version, OZ 2.0, aims to narrow the focus of tax incentives to lower-income and rural areas, reducing the number of eligible zones from 8,764 to an estimated 6,544. Citywide ballot measures pitch faster permitting, procurement

The Real Deal — New York — Tue, 21 Jul 2026 19:26:46 +0000

SUMMARY

Infrastructure Investment / Government Policy

New York City's Commission on Government Efficiency (COGE) is proposing several ballot measures to streamline the city's permitting and procurement processes, aiming to accelerate construction and contract awards. Key proposals include establishing a central hub for construction permits across multiple agencies, delegating certificate of occupancy authority, and centralizing waterfront permitting under the Department of Buildings. Additionally, measures are proposed to simplify the procurement of goods and services for small businesses and nonprofits, and to expedite the repurposing of small public land lots and the leasing of office space for municipal use. These changes are intended to support the Mamdani administration's goal of creating and preserving 400,000 affordable housing units over the next decade by reducing bureaucratic delays.

Small Parcels, Big Demand: How O’Hare keeps outperforming Chicago’s industrial market

REJournals — Wed, 22 Jul 2026 10:03:00 +0000

SUMMARY

Opinion / Market Commentary

The O'Hare industrial submarket in Chicago continues to outperform the broader metropolitan industrial market, driven by a scarcity of large development parcels that limits new supply. This constraint keeps vacancy rates low, around 5.4-5.5%, compared to 8.5-9% in the wider suburban market. Demand is concentrated in smaller industrial spaces, typically 10,000 to 50,000 square feet, attracting air freight, pharmaceutical, and last-mile logistics users who value the central location. Brokers anticipate that rents will remain high and continue to grow due to persistent demand and the lack of developable land, with new supply focusing on infill locations and leveraging favorable tax treatment.
