Big tech still values the office, but in a different way

REJournals — Wed, 01 Jul 2026 10:04:00 +0000

SUMMARY

Market Research / Office Strategy

A new report from Newmark indicates that major technology companies are reshaping their office footprints, focusing on fewer buildings, better locations, and higher-quality workplaces. The study, "Big Tech's U.S. Office Strategy Is Clear: Concentrate, Upgrade, Expand," reveals that the 100 largest office leases by U.S. tech occupiers over the past year totaled 16.5 million square feet across just 14 markets, with an average deal size of 165,000 square feet. This trend is largely driven by artificial intelligence firms and a desire to be near top talent and venture capital sources, particularly in the San Francisco Bay Area, New York City, and Boston. The report highlights that 83% of these leases were in top-tier properties, with most companies expanding their footprints rather than contracting, and lease terms are lengthening, signaling a continued commitment to physical office space as a strategic asset.

The Housing Bill Sitting On Trump's Desk Isn't The Game-Changer Many Hoped — Or Feared

Bisnow News Feed — Tue, 30 Jun 2026 13:48:49 -0400

SUMMARY

Opinion / Market Commentary

The 21st Century Road to Housing Act, currently awaiting presidential signature, is considered a significant housing policy, though experts believe it will not be a game-changer for the commercial real estate sector. While it includes provisions to streamline environmental reviews, offer grants for increased housing supply, and remove the permanent chassis mandate for manufactured housing to reduce costs, its impact on the broader multifamily market is expected to be limited. The bill also raises the public welfare investment cap for banks and reauthorizes HUD's HOME Program, which are seen as positive signals for affordable housing development. However, some industry leaders view these measures as incremental rather than transformative, with one CEO noting they are more of a "tangent" for multifamily clients. The legislation also includes a cap on institutional ownership of single-family homes, though experts suggest there are exemptions and workarounds, particularly for new build-to-rent construction.

Rent Guidelines Board Vote Sends New York’s Landlords Into Uncharted Territory

Commercial Observer — Tue, 30 Jun 2026 19:34:52 +0000

SUMMARY

Opinion / Market Commentary

The New York City Rent Guidelines Board (RGB) voted 7-1 to freeze rents for one- and two-year leases on rent-stabilized apartments, a decision that has drawn strong criticism from landlord advocacy groups and legal experts. Critics argue that Mayor Zohran Mamdani's recent appointments to the RGB predetermined the outcome, disregarding statutory requirements for data-driven decisions. Landlord representatives contend that without rent increases, property owners cannot afford to maintain rent-stabilized units, and lawsuits are expected to challenge the legality of the freeze. Conversely, proponents of the freeze cite RGB data indicating a 6.2% increase in net operating income for landlords of rent-stabilized units, suggesting owners can absorb the freeze while tenants face significant financial burdens.

Restoring An Industrial Core The Nonprofit Way: What Buffalo’s Northland Corridor Can Teach Other Cities

REBusinessOnline — Wed, 01 Jul 2026 11:54:00 +0000

SUMMARY

Opinion / Market Commentary

This article argues that nonprofit-led redevelopment is a crucial strategy for revitalizing distressed industrial areas where traditional for-profit developers are hesitant to invest due to limited ROI potential. The author, an executive at Buffalo Urban Development Corp., highlights that nonprofits prioritize community outcomes, have access to diverse public funding, and can undertake complex, long-term projects like environmental remediation that for-profit entities often avoid. The Northland Corridor in Buffalo serves as a case study, demonstrating how a phased, mission-driven approach can create conditions for future private investment by focusing on workforce development and strategic site control, ultimately rebuilding economic drivers and fostering community trust.
