Elliman’s AI overhaul raises questions about future of agents

The Real Deal — New York — Sat, 11 Jul 2026 13:00:00 +0000

SUMMARY

Opinion / Market Commentary

Douglas Elliman's recent announcement of a technology overhaul, powered by Google Cloud AI, has ignited a debate within the residential real estate industry regarding the future of agents. CEO Michael Liebowitz indicated that these advancements would lead to layoffs and a reduced need for agents, echoing trends seen in other sectors. While AI tools are helping agents with administrative tasks like writing listing descriptions and drafting contracts, some brokers report client frustration due to AI-generated, often inaccurate, price expectations. Despite these challenges, agents largely believe their roles remain essential, successfully navigating client pushback and demonstrating the continued necessity of human expertise in dealmaking.

Why this Brooklyn project is nowhere to be seen

The Real Deal — New York — Sat, 11 Jul 2026 13:00:00 +0000

SUMMARY

Opinion / Market Commentary

This opinion piece argues that a proposed 250-unit multifamily development in Brooklyn, approved 17 months ago, remains stalled due to a confluence of economic and political factors. The author contends that elevated interest rates, the high percentage of affordable units promised, and a high wage scale mandate for large projects have made financing challenging for the developer. Pfizer building scare tests office-to-resi thesis

The Real Deal — New York — Sat, 11 Jul 2026 12:00:00 +0000

SUMMARY

Opinion / Market Commentary

A structural failure at the former Pfizer headquarters in Midtown Manhattan, currently undergoing an office-to-residential conversion, has highlighted the inherent risks and complexities of adaptive reuse projects. While the incident caused a temporary halt in construction and prompted evacuations, the developer anticipates only a few weeks of delay, maintaining a 2027 completion target for the 1,600-apartment project. This event underscores the challenges of retrofitting decades-old office buildings for residential use, which often involves extensive structural modifications and can introduce unexpected complications, potentially leading to increased engineering reviews, tighter underwriting, and higher costs for projects already facing elevated construction and financing expenses. Despite these challenges, the broader office-to-residential conversion trend in New York City is expected to continue, supported by expanded tax incentives and loosened zoning rules aimed at addressing both high office vacancies and the housing shortage.
