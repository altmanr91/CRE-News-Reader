Treasury Selloff Signals Real-Rate Shock, Not Inflation Panic

Connect CRE — Wed, 30 Sep 2026 11:57:36 +0000

SUMMARY

Opinion / Market Commentary

The recent sharp selloff in the Treasury market, which saw 10-year yields reach their highest level since 2007, is primarily a repricing of real rates and policy expectations rather than a sign of inflation panic. Despite strong U.S. business activity data and a weak five-year note auction, 10-year inflation breakeven rates have remained stable, indicating that investors are demanding higher compensation for real growth, capital scarcity, and duration risk. The market's expectation is that monetary policy will remain restrictive for longer, coupled with stronger economic growth and increased competition for capital, including significant demand from AI infrastructure spending. This dynamic suggests a continued risk of higher long-end yields and cross-asset volatility, with a plausible stress scenario of the 10-year yield moving towards 5.50%.

Mamdani, Hochul Secure Stay in Court Order Against Pied-à-Terre Tax Rollout

Commercial Observer — Wed, 30 Sep 2026 14:31:51 +0000

SUMMARY

CRE Legal / Regulatory

A Staten Island judge has temporarily halted the rollout of New York City's controversial pied-à-terre tax, ruling that the city mishandled its implementation. The tax, championed by Mayor Zohran Mamdani and Governor Kathy Hochul, aims to generate $500 million annually from second homes valued over $5 million. The city has filed an appeal, invoking an automatic stay, allowing the tax to proceed for now, but the ruling may force a revised approach to identifying and notifying affected homeowners. Real estate industry groups, including the Real Estate Board of New York, have praised the judge's decision, citing concerns about the tax's legality and fairness, while city officials maintain the tax is a necessary measure for the ultra-wealthy to contribute their fair share to city operations.
