Secrecy Is Killing Data Center Deals

Commercial Observer — Fri, 03 Jul 2026 15:00:42 +0000

SUMMARY

Opinion / Market Commentary

The data center industry's reliance on secrecy, through non-disclosure agreements and redacted filings, is causing significant project delays and cancellations due to community opposition. This lack of transparency, often justified as protecting proprietary information, backfires by eroding trust and fueling resistance when communities feel excluded from key decisions. The author argues that transparency should be viewed as a strategic development tool, not a concession, as early and consistent disclosure of foundational information like power demand, water use, and site plans leads to smoother approvals and fewer challenges. The current fragmented disclosure practices create an environment of resistance and risk for developers, highlighting the need for a standardized framework to ensure projects are evaluated on their merits rather than rejected on principle.

Land Use Intelligence Is Increasingly a Must-Have in Deal-Making

Commercial Observer — Fri, 03 Jul 2026 12:00:48 +0000

SUMMARY

Opinion / Market Commentary

This article argues that land use intelligence, particularly zoning data, must be integrated earlier into the commercial real estate underwriting process to mitigate costly mistakes. The author contends that while financial models are sophisticated, underlying assumptions about property characteristics and local regulations often lead to errors because zoning information is fragmented and traditionally treated as a downstream diligence item. By leveraging new technology and AI to centralize and connect land use data with other datasets, investors, developers, and lenders can make more informed decisions, identify risks sooner, and improve deal outcomes.

Blackstone's QTS Abandons Digital Gateway Project, Ends Legal Fight

Bisnow News Feed — Fri, 03 Jul 2026 10:06:07 -0400

SUMMARY

Development / Project Abandonment

Blackstone's data center arm, QTS, has officially withdrawn from the ambitious Digital Gateway project in Prince William County, Virginia, and ceased its legal battle to advance the development. This decision follows a lower court ruling that voided the necessary rezoning approvals, and the company determined that further litigation was not worthwhile. The project, which aimed to create one of the world's largest data center clusters, faced significant opposition from local landowners and advocacy groups.
