MARKET: SOUTH FLORIDA

South Florida Dirt: The story behind a closed loophole in latest Live Local tweak bill

The Real Deal — Miami — Sun, 05 Jul 2026 17:00:00 +0000

SUMMARY

CRE Legal / Regulatory

Florida's recently enacted House Bill 1389, a tweak to the Live Local Act, expands the definition of "person" under fair housing law to include government entities, aiming to deter local challenges to affordable housing developments. This change effectively removes sovereign immunity as a defense for cities and counties attempting to block such projects. The bill also broadens the types of land eligible for Live Local Act incentives, including properties owned by counties, municipalities, school districts, and religious institutions, regardless of existing zoning. Additionally, it prohibits counties from using setbacks or stepbacks to restrict building height, further empowering developers to create workforce and affordable housing.

How a courtroom showdown in Chicago could reshape residential real estate

The Real Deal — National — Sun, 05 Jul 2026 13:00:00 +0000

SUMMARY

CRE Legal / Regulatory

A federal courtroom in Chicago is the battleground for a significant legal dispute between Zillow, Compass, and Midwest Real Estate Data (MRED), which could redefine residential real estate practices. The core issue revolves around Zillow's policy of excluding listings that were publicly marketed elsewhere before appearing on the MLS, a move seen as a counter to Compass's strategy of private listings. Zillow argues that Compass and MRED are attempting to restrict inventory access, while Compass and MRED contend that Zillow is leveraging its market dominance to dictate listing practices. The outcome of this case is expected to have a profound impact on how brokerages, MLSs, and portals nationwide approach private listings and inventory access.

MARKET: MINNEAPOLIS-ST. PAUL, MN

A stable market? That’s how Colliers refers to Twin Cities industrial sector in latest research

REJournals — Mon, 06 Jul 2026 09:53:00 +0000

SUMMARY

Market Research / Industrial Report

The Minneapolis-St. Paul industrial market demonstrated stability in the second quarter of 2026, characterized by sustained tenant demand, particularly from large users and third-party logistics companies. While new supply has led to a slight increase in vacancy rates as recently completed projects lease up, the market recorded 1.05 million square feet of net absorption in Q2, bringing the year-to-date total to over 2.1 million square feet. A significant portion of the expanding development pipeline, which now exceeds 6.7 million square feet under construction, consists of pre-leased build-to-suit projects, mitigating concerns about oversupply. Average asking rents remained stable at $9.43 per square foot, supported by high replacement costs and limited competition among newer properties, while investment sales activity remained active and selective, totaling $282 million across 58 transactions.

MARKET INTELLIGENCE

The Twin Cities industrial market benefits from strong tenant demand, particularly from larger users and third-party logistics companies. Vacancy rates are ticking higher due to new supply outpacing leasing activity in some submarkets, with South Central and Southwest submarkets experiencing more pronounced pressures. Conversely, North Central and Northwest submarkets show stable fundamentals with lower available space and limited construction. Sublease availability reached its highest level in the current market cycle at 4.3 million square feet. Investment activity remains selective, favoring well-located logistics facilities, while older buildings face pricing pressure.
